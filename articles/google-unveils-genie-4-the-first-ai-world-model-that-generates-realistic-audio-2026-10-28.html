Google Unveils Genie 4, the First AI World Model That Generates Realistic Audio

2026-10-28 · 3 min read
Google’s new Genie 4 world model can create lifelike soundscapes, marking a milestone in immersive AI experiences.

Google has announced the launch of Genie 4, its latest world model that can generate audio as well as visual content. The new system builds on the company’s previous work in 3D world generation and adds the ability to produce realistic soundscapes that match the visual environment. According to the company, Genie 4 can create audio that reacts to changes in lighting, weather, and movement, making it a powerful tool for developers building virtual worlds, games, and training simulations.

What Genie 4 Brings to the Table

Genie 4 is the first world model that can generate audio that feels natural and responsive. While earlier models focused on rendering 3D scenes and textures, this version adds a new dimension: sound. The system can produce ambient noises, footsteps, and environmental effects that change in real time as the virtual scene evolves. This capability opens up new possibilities for immersive storytelling, interactive training, and realistic virtual reality experiences.

Developer Tools and Integration

Google has made Genie 4 available through its AI platform, allowing developers to integrate the model into their own applications. The company has also announced a new audio recorder feature for Gemini Notebook, its AI-powered note-taking app. The recorder can capture spoken input and feed it directly into Genie 4, enabling developers to create audio-visual content from a single source. The combination of audio recording and world generation streamlines the workflow for creators who want to build rich, interactive environments.

Use Cases Across Industries

Manufacturing and training simulations stand to benefit from Genie 4’s realistic audio. In industrial settings, sound cues can help operators recognize equipment status or detect anomalies. The model’s ability to simulate realistic noise can also improve safety training by replicating the auditory environment of hazardous sites. Beyond manufacturing, game developers can use Genie 4 to create more immersive worlds, while educators can build interactive lessons that respond to student actions with appropriate sound cues.

Looking Ahead

Google’s announcement signals a broader trend toward multimodal AI systems that combine visual, auditory, and textual data. As developers gain access to tools like Genie 4, we can expect a wave of new applications that leverage realistic sound to enhance user engagement. The company’s focus on making the model accessible through its AI platform suggests that Genie 4 will become a staple in the toolkit of creators looking to push the boundaries of immersive digital experiences.
