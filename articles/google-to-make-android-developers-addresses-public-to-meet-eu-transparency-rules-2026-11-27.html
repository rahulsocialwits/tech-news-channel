Google to Make Android Developers’ Addresses Public to Meet EU Transparency Rules

2026-11-27 · 3 min read
Google will publish the physical addresses of all Android app developers worldwide as part of its compliance with new European privacy regulations.

Google announced that it will begin publishing the physical addresses of every Android developer listed in the Play Store. The move is aimed at complying with the European Union’s new transparency requirements that demand companies disclose the real‑world locations of the people behind the apps they offer.

Why the change matters

Under the EU’s Digital Services Act, app platforms must provide clear information about the entities that create and maintain the software they host. By making developer addresses public, Google intends to give users and regulators a way to verify who is responsible for an app and to hold them accountable if necessary.

How it will work

Developers will see their address listed in the public profile of their app on the Play Store. The data will be available in a searchable format, allowing anyone to look up a developer’s location. Google said the change will not affect the privacy of developers’ personal data beyond the address itself.

Reactions from the community

Some developers expressed concern that the move could expose them to unwanted attention or security risks. Others welcomed the transparency, arguing it could help users make more informed choices about the apps they download.

Next steps

Google plans to roll out the feature in the coming weeks, with an initial focus on developers in the European Economic Area. The company will monitor the impact and adjust the implementation as needed to balance transparency with privacy and safety.
