Mecka AI Secures $60M to Fuel Humanoid Robot Training

2026-10-08 · 3 min read
Robot data startup Mecka AI raises $60 million from Sequoia to expand its motion‑capture platform for training humanoid robots.

In a move that underscores the growing demand for high‑quality human motion data, the robot‑data startup Mecka AI announced a $60 million funding round led by Sequoia Capital. The capital will help the company scale its platform, which pays participants to record everyday tasks and then uses the footage to train humanoid robots and other robotic systems.

How Mecka AI’s Platform Works

Mecka AI’s core offering is a cloud‑based data collection service. Users—ranging from office workers to athletes—record routine activities such as typing, walking, or cooking. The videos are anonymized and uploaded to a secure repository where machine‑learning models analyze the motion patterns. The resulting datasets are then sold to robotics firms that need realistic human motion for training and testing their robots.

Why Motion Data Matters for Robotics

Robots that can mimic human movements are essential for applications from manufacturing to elder care. However, creating realistic motion requires vast amounts of high‑quality data. Traditional datasets are often limited in scope or expensive to produce. Mecka AI’s model of paying participants to capture real‑world tasks offers a scalable solution that can generate diverse, high‑fidelity motion sequences.

Sequoia’s Confidence in the Market

Sequoia Capital, a long‑time investor in AI and robotics, highlighted the startup’s unique approach to data collection and its potential to become a critical infrastructure provider for the robotics ecosystem. The funding round also included participation from other venture firms that specialize in AI and automation.

Future Plans and Impact

With the new capital, Mecka AI plans to expand its user base, enhance its data‑processing pipelines, and develop tools that allow robotics companies to integrate the datasets more seamlessly into their training workflows. The company also aims to broaden the range of tasks captured, including more complex, multi‑person interactions.

Industry Context

Mecka AI’s success comes amid a broader trend of startups focusing on data as a service for AI and robotics. Similar efforts are emerging in areas such as autonomous driving, where companies are building large, annotated datasets to train perception models. The funding round signals that investors see a clear path to monetizing high‑quality motion data for the next generation of robots.
