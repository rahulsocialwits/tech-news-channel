Security Teams Struggle to Keep Software Flaws in Check

2026-11-18 · 3 min read
Backlog of software security findings grows as teams debate prioritization, according to Black Duck report.

Security teams across the industry are facing a widening gap between the number of software vulnerabilities discovered and the resources available to fix them. A recent report from Black Duck highlights a growing backlog of security findings that is outpacing the capacity of many organizations to remediate them.

Backlog on the Rise

The report notes that the volume of identified software flaws has increased steadily over the past year, while the time required to triage and address each issue remains largely unchanged. This mismatch has led to a backlog that is becoming harder to manage, especially for teams that rely on manual processes to decide which vulnerabilities to tackle first.

Debate Over Prioritization

Experts disagree on how much time should be spent evaluating the severity and impact of each flaw versus the actual remediation work. Some argue that a more automated, data‑driven approach could help teams focus on the most critical risks, while others caution that over‑automation might miss context‑specific nuances.

Implications for Businesses

As the backlog grows, the risk of exploitation increases. Companies that cannot keep pace with patching may find themselves vulnerable to attacks that could compromise sensitive data or disrupt operations. The report calls for a reassessment of resource allocation and the adoption of more efficient workflows to close the gap between discovery and resolution.
