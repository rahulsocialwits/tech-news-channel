GlobalFoundries Unveils FDX Fusion: 7nm-Class Performance Without EUV

2026-11-07 · 3 min read
GlobalFoundries announced its FDX Fusion process, delivering 7nm-class performance using planar FD‑SOI transistors and no EUV lithography, targeting high‑volume production by 2030.

GlobalFoundries (GF) has revealed new details about its FDX Fusion technology, a next‑generation process that promises 7nm‑class performance and 8nm‑class logic density without the need for extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography. The company said the approach uses planar fully depleted silicon‑on‑insulator (FD‑SOI) transistors, a departure from the industry’s reliance on EUV for sub‑10nm nodes.

FDX Fusion’s Technical Highlights

According to GF’s VP, the FDX Fusion node achieves performance comparable to 7nm processes while maintaining the lower cost and higher yield benefits of planar technology. The design leverages advanced patterning techniques and a refined process flow that eliminates the EUV step, which is a significant cost driver in current semiconductor manufacturing.

GF also announced that the FDX program will include planar transistors in addition to the FD‑SOI devices, broadening the technology’s applicability across a range of logic and memory products. The company plans to begin high‑volume manufacturing of the FDX Fusion node by 2030, positioning it as a long‑term solution for customers seeking high performance without the EUV expense.

Implications for the Industry

By sidestepping EUV, GF’s FDX Fusion could reduce the barrier to entry for fabs that lack EUV infrastructure, potentially accelerating the adoption of advanced nodes in emerging markets. The technology also aligns with the broader industry trend toward more cost‑effective, high‑performance process nodes as the demand for power‑efficient chips grows.

While the announcement did not include specific performance benchmarks or yield data, the emphasis on 7nm‑class performance suggests that GF aims to compete with leading-edge nodes from other foundries. The company’s focus on planar FD‑SOI transistors also indicates a strategic shift toward more mature, reliable manufacturing techniques.

Next Steps for GlobalFoundries

GF plans to continue refining the FDX Fusion process, with a roadmap that includes additional planar transistor options and further optimization of the FD‑SOI design. The company’s goal is to deliver a high‑volume, high‑yield process that can meet the needs of both high‑performance and power‑efficient applications.

As the semiconductor industry faces supply chain constraints and rising manufacturing costs, GF’s FDX Fusion offers a potential pathway to maintain performance gains while controlling expenses. The technology’s success will depend on its ability to deliver on performance promises and achieve the projected 2030 high‑volume target.
