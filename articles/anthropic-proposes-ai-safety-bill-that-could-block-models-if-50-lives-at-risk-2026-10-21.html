Anthropic Proposes AI Safety Bill That Could Block Models If 50 Lives at Risk

2026-10-21 · 3 min read
Anthropic’s new policy would require a judge’s approval before federal agencies can block an AI model that could kill 50 or more people.

Anthropic, the San Francisco‑based AI research company, has unveiled a policy proposal that could reshape how federal agencies regulate potentially dangerous artificial intelligence systems. The draft bill would allow a judge to block the release of an AI model if it is deemed capable of causing the death of 50 or more people. The measure is aimed at preventing runaway AI deployments while preserving the ability of the public sector to intervene when necessary.

Key Provisions

The policy would require federal agencies to seek judicial authorization before they can halt a model’s deployment. This is a departure from current practice, where agencies can block releases directly. The proposal also includes a threshold of 50 potential fatalities as a trigger for judicial review.

Implications for the AI Landscape

By tying the ability to block a model to a court decision, Anthropic is pushing for a more formalized oversight mechanism. The move could set a precedent for other AI firms and regulators, potentially leading to a new standard for safety reviews in the industry.

Industry Reactions

Reactions to the proposal have been mixed. Some experts argue that judicial involvement could slow down necessary safety interventions, while others see it as a necessary check against unchecked AI proliferation.
