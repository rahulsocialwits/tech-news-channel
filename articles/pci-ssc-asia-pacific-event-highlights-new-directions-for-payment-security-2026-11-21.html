PCI SSC Asia‑Pacific Event Highlights New Directions for Payment Security

2026-11-21 · 3 min read
Industry leaders gather in New Delhi to discuss evolving payment security standards and emerging threats at the PCI Security Standards Council Asia‑Pacific event.

On November 20, 2026, the PCI Security Standards Council (PCI SSC) convened its Asia‑Pacific event in New Delhi, bringing together a roster of industry leaders, security experts, and regulatory bodies to chart the future of payment security. The gathering, which ran through the afternoon, focused on the latest developments in payment card security, emerging threat vectors, and the council’s roadmap for next‑generation standards.

Key Themes from the Conference

The event underscored three primary themes: the need for tighter controls around tokenization, the rise of AI‑driven fraud detection, and the importance of cross‑border regulatory alignment. Speakers highlighted how the evolving threat landscape—particularly the proliferation of sophisticated phishing campaigns and ransomware targeting payment processors—demands a more proactive and collaborative approach.

5Tattva’s Participation and Insights

Indian CERT‑In empanelled and PCI‑qualified security assessor 5Tattva took a prominent role in the discussions. The company’s representatives emphasized the importance of continuous monitoring and rapid incident response. They also showcased their latest suite of compliance tools designed to help merchants meet PCI DSS requirements more efficiently, especially in the context of the upcoming PCI SSC 4.0 release.

Emerging Standards and Compliance Roadmap

Attendees were briefed on the council’s upcoming PCI SSC 4.0 framework, which introduces stricter requirements for data encryption, secure software development life cycles, and enhanced monitoring of third‑party vendors. The new standard also expands the scope of the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS) to cover emerging payment methods such as contactless and mobile wallets.

Collaboration Across Borders

One of the event’s highlights was a panel discussion on harmonizing payment security regulations across the Asia‑Pacific region. Participants from the Reserve Bank of India, the Monetary Authority of Singapore, and the Australian Securities and Investments Commission shared best practices for aligning national regulations with PCI SSC guidelines. The goal is to reduce fragmentation and ensure that merchants operating across borders can maintain a consistent security posture.

Looking Ahead

As the payment ecosystem continues to evolve, the PCI SSC Asia‑Pacific event served as a reminder that security must keep pace with innovation. With the upcoming release of PCI SSC 4.0 and the growing adoption of AI‑based fraud detection, organizations will need to invest in both technology and talent to stay ahead of attackers. The event concluded with a call to action for all stakeholders to collaborate more closely, share threat intelligence, and adopt a zero‑trust mindset across the payment value chain.
