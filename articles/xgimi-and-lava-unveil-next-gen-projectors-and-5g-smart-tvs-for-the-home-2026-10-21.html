XGIMI and Lava Unveil Next‑Gen Projectors and 5G Smart TVs for the Home

2026-10-21 · 4 min read
XGIMI launches its Aura 3 Pro and Max 4K projectors on Kickstarter, while Lava introduces the Aura Curve 5G TV with a 6,000‑mAh battery and 45W charging support.

In a week of exciting home‑tech releases, two companies are pushing the boundaries of what can be projected and streamed in living rooms. XGIMI, a Chinese projector maker, has opened a Kickstarter campaign for its new Aura 3 Pro and Max 4K models, promising brighter images and a more immersive viewing experience. At the same time, Indian electronics firm Lava has rolled out the Aura Curve 5G, a mid‑segment smart TV that comes with a sizable battery and fast charging, positioning itself as a versatile entertainment hub for the next decade.

XGIMI’s Aura 3 Pro and Max 4K: Brightness Meets Portability

According to the company’s Kickstarter page, the Aura 3 Pro is a 1,200‑lumens projector that can deliver a 120‑inch image from just 1.5 meters away. The Max 4K variant ups the brightness to 1,500 lumens and adds a 4K resolution, making it suitable for both home cinema and gaming setups. XGIMI emphasizes the use of a new LED‑backlight technology that reduces power consumption while maintaining color accuracy. The projectors also feature a built‑in speaker system and support for HDR10, allowing users to enjoy high‑dynamic‑range content without external audio equipment.

Backers can choose between a standard shipping option and a premium package that includes a wall‑mount kit and a carrying case. XGIMI has set a funding goal of $1.2 million, with a stretch goal that would add a wireless audio transmitter for Bluetooth headphones. The company claims that the Aura line will be available for delivery in early 2027, pending regulatory approvals in key markets.

Lava’s Aura Curve 5G: A Battery‑Powered Smart TV

Lava’s new Aura Curve 5G is aimed at mid‑segment buyers looking for a flexible, high‑performance TV. The 55‑inch model ships with a 6,000‑mAh battery that can keep the screen running for up to three hours on a single charge, a feature that could appeal to users who want to avoid constant wall‑power connections. The TV also supports up to 45W charging, and it comes with a 33W charger in the box, allowing for quick top‑ups.

In addition to its power features, the Aura Curve 5G offers 5G connectivity, enabling streaming of high‑definition content without the need for a separate router. The device runs on Lava’s custom Android TV OS, which includes a curated app store and voice‑control integration with Google Assistant. The company has highlighted the TV’s curved display, which it claims reduces glare and improves viewing angles compared to flat panels.

Why These Releases Matter

Both XGIMI and Lava are addressing a growing demand for portable, high‑quality media devices. XGIMI’s projectors cater to consumers who want a cinema‑like experience without the bulk of a traditional projector, while Lava’s battery‑powered TV offers a solution for households that lack reliable power infrastructure or prefer a more flexible setup.

From a market perspective, the projector segment has seen a resurgence as streaming services expand their libraries and consumers look for ways to bring the theater home. Meanwhile, the smart TV market is increasingly competitive, with manufacturers racing to add features like 5G, battery backup, and AI‑driven content recommendations. By combining these innovations, XGIMI and Lava are positioning themselves as leaders in the next wave of home entertainment technology.

What’s Next?

Both companies have hinted at future developments. XGIMI plans to release a portable version of the Aura line that can be used outdoors, while Lava is exploring partnerships with local telecom providers to offer bundled 5G plans. As the industry moves toward more flexible, power‑efficient devices, consumers can expect further innovations that blur the line between traditional home appliances and mobile tech.
