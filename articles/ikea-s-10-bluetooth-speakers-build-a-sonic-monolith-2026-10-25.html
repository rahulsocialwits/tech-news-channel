IKEA’s $10 Bluetooth Speakers Build a Sonic Monolith

2026-10-25 · 3 min read
WIRED documents how 150 IKEA speakers were wired together to create a massive wall of sound, proving that cheap hardware can deliver impressive audio when scaled.

In a bold experiment that turns a budget-friendly retail brand into a sonic powerhouse, a team of DIY enthusiasts wired together 150 of IKEA’s inexpensive Bluetooth speakers to create a wall of sound that could fill a concert hall. The project, detailed by WIRED, demonstrates how mass‑produced, low‑cost audio devices can be repurposed into a large‑scale sound system when combined in large numbers.

From $10 to a Sonic Monolith

IKEA’s speakers, each priced at just $10, are designed for casual use in small rooms. They feature a single driver and a basic Bluetooth receiver, making them ideal for budget home audio setups. The team, however, took a different approach: they connected the speakers in series and parallel configurations, using a custom power distribution board to supply consistent voltage across all units.

Engineering the Connection

The key to the project was the wiring scheme. By arranging the speakers in a grid and using a combination of series and parallel connections, the team maintained a balanced impedance that prevented any single speaker from being overloaded. They also added a small amplifier module to boost the signal, ensuring that the combined output remained clear and distortion‑free.

Sound Quality and Performance

Despite the speakers’ modest specifications, the resulting sound was surprisingly robust. The wall of 150 units produced a loud, evenly distributed audio field that could fill a large room or outdoor space. The team reported that the system could reach 90 decibels at a distance of 10 meters, a level comparable to many commercial sound systems.

Implications for DIY Audio

This experiment highlights the potential of low‑cost hardware when used creatively. While the setup requires significant effort and technical knowledge, it shows that large‑scale audio installations can be built without expensive equipment. The project also raises questions about the environmental impact of mass‑produced consumer electronics, as the speakers were eventually disassembled and recycled.

Future Possibilities

As the maker community continues to explore unconventional uses for everyday devices, projects like this one may inspire new approaches to audio engineering, home theater design, and even large‑scale public installations. The IKEA speaker wall demonstrates that with ingenuity, even the simplest components can be transformed into something extraordinary.
