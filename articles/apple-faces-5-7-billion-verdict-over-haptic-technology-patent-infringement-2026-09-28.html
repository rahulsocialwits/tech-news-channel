Apple Faces $5.7 Billion Verdict Over Haptic Technology Patent Infringement

2026-09-28 · 3 min read
U.S. jury rules Apple must pay record $5.7 billion for infringing Taction patents related to its Taptic Engine.

A U.S. jury has ordered Apple Inc. to pay a record $5.7 billion for infringing two Taction patents that cover the haptic technology used in its Taptic Engine. The decision, announced on Friday, marks the largest civil penalty ever imposed for a patent dispute involving haptic feedback systems.

What the verdict means for Apple

The Taptic Engine, which creates the subtle vibrations users feel when interacting with Apple devices, was found to infringe on patents that protect the technology for generating tapping vibrations. The judgment could impact Apple’s future product designs and its licensing strategy for haptic features.

Broader implications for the tech industry

Patent litigation has long been a battleground for technology companies, but the size of this award underscores the value of haptic technology in modern devices. Competitors that rely on similar vibration feedback may need to review their own patent portfolios to avoid costly infringement.

Next steps for Apple

Apple has not yet announced whether it will appeal the decision. If it chooses to challenge the ruling, the case could move through the appellate courts, potentially setting new precedents for how haptic technology patents are evaluated.
