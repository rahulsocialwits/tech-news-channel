YouTube’s AV2 Video Codec Rollout Promises Smoother Streaming

2026-10-25 · 3 min read
YouTube is preparing to launch its new AV2 codec, aiming to improve video quality and reduce buffering for users worldwide.

Video streaming giant YouTube is gearing up to introduce its next‑generation AV2 codec, a move that could significantly enhance playback quality and reduce buffering for millions of users. According to Android Authority, the rollout is slated for early 2027, with the company already testing the new format across a range of devices and network conditions.

What AV2 Brings to the Table

AV2, short for Advanced Video 2, is designed to deliver higher compression efficiency than its predecessor, AV1. Early tests suggest that AV2 can achieve comparable or better visual fidelity at roughly 30–40% lower bitrates. This translates to smoother streaming on congested networks and lower data usage for mobile users.

Testing and Deployment Strategy

YouTube’s approach involves a phased rollout, starting with a limited set of high‑profile channels and gradually expanding to the broader user base. The company is also working closely with hardware manufacturers to ensure that AV2 decoding is supported on a wide range of devices, from smartphones to smart TVs.

Impact on Content Creators and Viewers

For creators, AV2 means the ability to upload higher‑resolution content without incurring the same bandwidth costs. Viewers, on the other hand, can expect fewer buffering events and clearer picture quality, especially on mobile connections where data limits are a concern.

Industry Context

The move aligns with a broader industry push toward more efficient video codecs. While AV1 has seen adoption in browsers and streaming platforms, AV2’s improved compression is poised to become the new standard for high‑definition video delivery.
