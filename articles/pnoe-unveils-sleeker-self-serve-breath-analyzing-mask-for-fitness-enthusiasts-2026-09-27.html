PNOE Unveils Sleeker Self‑Serve Breath‑Analyzing Mask for Fitness Enthusiasts

2026-09-27 · 3 min read
Malden‑based startup PNOE launches a new self‑serve mask that measures VO₂ max and metabolic markers in eight minutes, targeting gym users.

Malden, Mass., startup PNOE is set to roll out a new version of its breath‑analyzing mask on October 1, aiming to bring lab‑grade metabolic testing into the hands of everyday fitness enthusiasts. The updated mask, described by TechCrunch as a “sleeker self‑serve version,” will allow users to measure VO₂ max and other metabolic markers in just eight minutes without the need for a trained operator.

From Bane‑Inspired Prototype to Practical Tool

PNOE’s original mask, which once bore an unfortunate resemblance to the comic‑book villain Bane, was designed to capture detailed breath data for athletes and researchers. The new iteration removes the bulkier design and incorporates a streamlined interface that can be used in gyms, home studios, or outdoor training sessions.

How the Mask Works

Users place the mask over their nose and mouth and begin a short, guided breathing session. Sensors inside the mask analyze the composition of exhaled air, calculating VO₂ max—a key indicator of aerobic fitness—and other metabolic markers such as lactate threshold and respiratory exchange ratio. The data is then transmitted to a companion app, where it can be compared against personal baselines or shared with coaches.

Target Market and Use Cases

PNOE positions the mask as a tool for “gym‑goers” who want quick, actionable insights into their cardiovascular health. By eliminating the need for a trained operator, the company hopes to democratize access to high‑quality physiological data. The eight‑minute test is short enough to fit into a typical workout routine, making it attractive for athletes who need frequent monitoring.

Business Model and Future Plans

While the article does not detail pricing, PNOE’s strategy appears to focus on subscription services for individual users and bulk licensing for fitness centers. The startup also hinted at future integrations with wearable devices and coaching platforms, potentially expanding its ecosystem beyond the mask itself.

Industry Context

The launch comes at a time when the fitness tech market is seeing a surge in wearables that track heart rate, sleep, and activity. PNOE’s breath‑analysis technology offers a more nuanced view of metabolic health, filling a niche that current consumer devices do not address. By providing lab‑grade data in a consumer‑friendly format, the company could set a new standard for at‑home fitness diagnostics.

Conclusion

PNOE’s self‑serve mask represents a significant step toward making sophisticated physiological testing accessible to the average gym‑goer. If the company can deliver on its promise of accurate, rapid results, it may well become a staple in fitness centers and personal training regimens worldwide.
