Microsoft’s 20‑Hour Outage Leaves Global Businesses in Turmoil

2026-11-13 · 3 min read
A worldwide outage of Microsoft’s cloud services, including Outlook and Teams, disrupted businesses and public services for nearly a day.

For nearly twenty hours, Microsoft’s cloud‑based services were down, sending a ripple of disruption through businesses, schools, and government agencies worldwide. The outage began in the early hours of Friday, 13 November, and was reported by multiple outlets as affecting core productivity tools such as Outlook, Teams, and other Office 365 services.

Impact on Global Operations

According to Engadget, the interruption was felt across a wide spectrum of users. The House of Representatives, health care providers, and educational institutions were among those that reported significant service disruptions. The outage also hit airline customer support systems, causing delays and confusion for travelers.

India’s Experience

In India, the impact was particularly pronounced. The Hindu noted that the disruption to Microsoft’s cloud‑based email service affected business operations and airline customer support, while several public services across the country reported problems. The scale of the outage underscored the reliance of critical infrastructure on Microsoft’s cloud platform.

Recovery and Response

Microsoft announced that services were returning to near‑normal levels after the outage. The company stated that the incident had been resolved and that it was working to prevent a recurrence. While the exact cause of the outage was not disclosed in the sources provided, the incident highlighted the vulnerability of large‑scale cloud services to unexpected failures.

Lessons for the Cloud Industry

Incidents of this magnitude serve as a reminder of the importance of robust redundancy, rapid incident response, and transparent communication with users. As businesses increasingly depend on cloud‑based productivity tools, the need for resilient infrastructure and clear escalation procedures becomes ever more critical.
