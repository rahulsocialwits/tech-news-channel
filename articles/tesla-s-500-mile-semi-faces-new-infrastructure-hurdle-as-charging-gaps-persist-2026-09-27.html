Tesla’s 500‑mile Semi Faces New Infrastructure Hurdle as Charging Gaps Persist

2026-09-27 · 3 min read
Tesla’s long‑range electric truck still struggles to find enough charging points, limiting its commercial viability.

Tesla’s ambitious 500‑mile Semi, unveiled last year as the company’s flagship electric truck, has hit a new roadblock: the charging network still lags behind the vehicle’s range. While the Semi can theoretically travel half a thousand miles on a single charge, the reality on the road is that charging stations are unevenly distributed, especially along major freight corridors.

Charging gaps keep the Semi from reaching its full potential

According to a recent analysis, the Semi’s 500‑mile range is only achievable if a driver can locate a compatible charger every 200 miles or so. However, the current infrastructure in the United States provides fewer than 200 fast‑charging stations per 1,000 miles of interstate, meaning trucks often have to detour or wait for a charger to become available.

Impact on commercial fleets

For logistics companies, the lack of a dense charging network translates into higher operating costs and longer delivery times. Even with Tesla’s Supercharger network, which offers 250 kW charging speeds, the Semi’s 500‑mile range is only practical if chargers are strategically placed along key routes.

What’s next for Tesla?

Tesla is reportedly working on expanding its Supercharger network, but the company must also collaborate with state and federal agencies to accelerate the deployment of public charging infrastructure. Until the network catches up, the Semi’s promise of zero‑emission freight will remain largely theoretical.
