Microsoft 365 Global Outage Traced to Three Combined Failures

2026-11-17 · 3 min read
Microsoft explains how a hidden bug, traffic shift and capacity drop caused a worldwide Microsoft 365 outage.

Microsoft has released a detailed post‑mortem on the global outage that knocked out Microsoft 365 services for several hours on Monday. The company identified three interlocking problems that cascaded into a full‑scale failure.

Hidden Bug in Traffic Routing

The first trigger was a software bug that misrouted traffic within the company’s internal load‑balancing system. The bug caused requests to be sent to a small subset of servers that were not prepared to handle the load.

Shifted Traffic Amplified the Issue

As the misdirected traffic piled up, Microsoft’s traffic‑shifting mechanisms kicked in to redistribute the load. Instead of balancing evenly, the shift concentrated traffic on a few under‑provisioned nodes, creating a bottleneck.

Reduced Capacity Exposed the Vulnerability

Compounding the problem, a recent capacity reduction in the affected data centers left those nodes with less memory and CPU than required. The combination of the bug, traffic shift and reduced capacity triggered cascading failures across the Microsoft 365 stack.

Lessons for Cloud Operators

Microsoft’s analysis underscores the importance of robust fail‑over testing, real‑time capacity monitoring, and clear traffic‑routing policies. The company has pledged to review its incident response procedures and to implement additional safeguards to prevent a similar event in the future.
