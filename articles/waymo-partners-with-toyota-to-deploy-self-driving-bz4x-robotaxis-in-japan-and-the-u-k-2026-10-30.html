Waymo Partners with Toyota to Deploy Self‑Driving bZ4X Robotaxis in Japan and the U.K.

2026-10-30 · 3 min read
Waymo will begin testing autonomous Toyota bZ4X electric vehicles in Japan next year as part of a new partnership that expands its robotaxi network.

Waymo, the self‑driving arm of Alphabet, has announced a new partnership with Toyota that will see the company begin testing autonomous versions of the Japanese automaker’s electric bZ4X SUV in Japan next year. The deal also extends to the United Kingdom, where Waymo plans to deploy the same robotaxi platform in the coming months.

What the partnership means for Waymo’s robotaxi rollout

Under the agreement, Waymo will use Toyota’s bZ4X as the base vehicle for its autonomous fleet. The bZ4X is a fully electric SUV that has already been sold in Japan and is slated for a wider release in the U.K. By leveraging Toyota’s existing production lines and vehicle architecture, Waymo can accelerate the deployment of its robotaxi service in two major markets.

Benefits for Toyota and the broader mobility ecosystem

For Toyota, the partnership offers a way to showcase its electric vehicle technology in a new context: autonomous, on‑demand transportation. The collaboration also provides Toyota with access to Waymo’s advanced software stack, which includes perception, planning and control systems that have been refined through years of testing in the United States.

Technical details of the autonomous bZ4X

Waymo’s autonomous system will be integrated into the bZ4X’s existing hardware, adding sensors such as LiDAR, radar and high‑definition cameras. The software will run on Waymo’s proprietary platform, which has been proven in a variety of driving environments. The partnership will also involve joint testing of the vehicle’s safety features, including emergency braking and lane‑keeping assistance.

Regulatory and market considerations

Both Japan and the U.K. have been working to create regulatory frameworks that allow autonomous vehicles to operate on public roads. Waymo’s entry into these markets will require compliance with local safety standards and data privacy regulations. The partnership with Toyota is expected to streamline the approval process, as the automaker already has established relationships with regulatory bodies in both countries.

Future outlook

Waymo’s move into Japan and the U.K. marks a significant step in the company’s global expansion strategy. By partnering with a local manufacturer, Waymo can reduce the time and cost associated with bringing its robotaxi service to new regions. The collaboration also signals a growing trend of automakers and autonomous technology firms working together to bring self‑driving services to consumers worldwide.
