AMD Unveils Gorgon Halo Mini‑PC with 192GB RAM for AI Workloads

2026-09-29 · 3 min read
AMD’s new Gorgon Halo mini‑PC delivers 192GB of RAM and a Ryzen AI Max+ Pro 495 processor, targeting high‑performance AI and data‑science tasks in a compact form factor.

AMD has announced the Gorgon Halo, a compact mini‑PC that packs an impressive 192GB of RAM and the company’s latest Ryzen AI Max+ Pro 495 processor. The system is aimed at developers and data‑science teams that need a small footprint without sacrificing performance for AI workloads.

Specs that set it apart

The Gorgon Halo is built around the Ryzen AI Max+ Pro 495, a chip that combines traditional CPU cores with integrated AI acceleration. Coupled with 192GB of DDR5 memory, the machine can handle large datasets and complex neural‑network models that would normally require a rack‑mounted server. The design also includes multiple PCIe 5.0 slots, allowing users to add high‑speed GPUs or storage devices as needed.

Price and availability

Early pricing for the Gorgon Halo starts at $7,099, a figure that reflects its high‑end specifications. AMD has positioned the device as a “mini‑PC” rather than a full‑size workstation, emphasizing its small form factor and low power consumption. The company plans to ship the first units in early 2027, with a limited early‑bird discount for customers who pre‑order.

Use cases

With its combination of CPU and AI cores, the Gorgon Halo is well suited for edge‑AI applications, real‑time data analytics, and rapid prototyping of machine‑learning models. The large memory pool also makes it attractive for researchers working on large‑scale simulations or training deep‑learning networks that require significant RAM bandwidth.

Industry context

AMD’s move follows a broader industry trend toward compact, high‑performance computing solutions. Companies like Intel and Nvidia have released similar mini‑PCs that target data‑center edge and AI workloads. By offering a device that can fit on a desk or in a server rack, AMD is expanding its portfolio beyond traditional desktop and workstation products.

What’s next?

AMD has not yet announced a full line of accessories or software support for the Gorgon Halo, but the company is expected to release a suite of development tools and pre‑built AI frameworks in the coming months. The launch of the device also signals AMD’s continued focus on AI acceleration, a sector that has seen rapid growth in recent years.
