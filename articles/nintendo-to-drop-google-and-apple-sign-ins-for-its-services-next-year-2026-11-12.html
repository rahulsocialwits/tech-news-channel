Nintendo to Drop Google and Apple Sign‑Ins for Its Services Next Year

2026-11-12 · 3 min read
Nintendo will no longer allow users to log in with Google or Apple accounts, shifting to its own authentication system by early 2027.

Starting in early 2027, Nintendo will discontinue support for Google and Apple sign‑ins across its services. The move, announced by the company on November 12, 2026, means that players will need to use Nintendo’s own authentication system to access games, the Nintendo Switch Online app, and other digital storefronts.

Why the change?

Nintendo cited security and user experience concerns as the primary reasons for the shift. By moving to a single, internally managed login, the company hopes to reduce the risk of account hijacking and streamline account recovery processes.

Impact on users

Gamers who currently use their Google or Apple credentials will need to create a new Nintendo account or link an existing one. The company has stated that account data, such as purchase history and cloud saves, will be migrated automatically to the new system.

What this means for the industry

The decision follows a broader trend of major platforms reevaluating third‑party authentication. While Google and Apple have long offered convenient single‑sign‑on options, some developers are moving toward proprietary solutions to gain tighter control over user data and security.
