Apple Unveils Touchscreen MacBook Pro, Pushing Laptop Prices to New Heights

2026-10-29 · 3 min read
Apple’s latest MacBook Pro features an OLED touchscreen and a price tag that could reach $14,549, marking the most expensive laptop ever released by the company.

Apple’s newest MacBook Pro, revealed at a Los Angeles event on Thursday, is the company’s first laptop to feature a touchscreen display. The device uses an organic light‑emitting diode (OLED) panel that offers vibrant colors and deep blacks, a technology that has long been reserved for the iPhone and iPad line‑ups. The move signals Apple’s intent to bring its premium design language to the portable computer market.

Price and Configuration

According to TechCrunch, the base model of the new MacBook Pro starts at $1,999 for a 14‑inch version and $2,499 for a 16‑inch version. However, the price climbs steeply as users add upgrades. A fully loaded 16‑inch MacBook Pro with a 12‑core Apple Silicon chip, 64 GB of unified memory, 8 TB of storage, and the highest‑end graphics option can reach a staggering $14,549. This figure makes it the most expensive laptop Apple has ever sold, surpassing even the high‑end iMacs and the Pro Display XDR.

Design and Features

The new MacBook Pro retains the thin, light chassis that has become a hallmark of Apple’s laptops, but the addition of a touchscreen introduces a new interaction paradigm. The OLED panel supports a 120‑Hz refresh rate and a 1,000‑nits peak brightness, which should benefit professionals who rely on color accuracy and smooth scrolling. The device also continues to use the Magic Keyboard with a refined scissor mechanism, and it includes the new M2‑Pro and M2‑Max silicon chips that deliver up to 40% faster CPU performance and 30% faster GPU performance compared to the previous generation.

Market Impact

Industry analysts note that the high price point may limit the MacBook Pro’s appeal to a niche segment of power users and creative professionals. The cost of the top‑tier configuration is comparable to high‑end gaming laptops from other manufacturers, but Apple’s brand premium and the integration of its silicon ecosystem give it a competitive edge. The touchscreen feature could also attract designers and video editors who prefer direct manipulation of UI elements.

Future Outlook

Apple’s decision to introduce a touchscreen MacBook Pro reflects a broader trend of converging device categories. While the company has historically avoided touchscreens on its laptops, the move aligns with its strategy to offer a unified experience across iPhone, iPad, and Mac. The company’s focus on high‑performance silicon and premium displays suggests that future iterations may further push the boundaries of mobile computing.
