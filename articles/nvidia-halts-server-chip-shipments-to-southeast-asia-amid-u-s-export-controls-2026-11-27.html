Nvidia Halts Server Chip Shipments to Southeast Asia Amid U.S. Export Controls

2026-11-27 · 3 min read
Nvidia has paused shipments of its server chips to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia as the U.S. tightens export controls to keep AI hardware out of China.

Nvidia announced today that it has instructed its manufacturing partners to pause all server‑chip shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia. The move comes as the United States ramps up its efforts to prevent advanced AI hardware from reaching China, tightening export controls on high‑performance computing components.

Why the pause matters

The decision follows a broader U.S. strategy to limit the flow of AI‑accelerating technology to China. By halting deliveries to key Southeast Asian markets, Nvidia is aligning with the new export‑control framework that targets the supply chain for AI chips.

Impact on the region

Companies in Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia that rely on Nvidia’s GPUs for data‑center workloads and AI research will need to seek alternative suppliers or delay new deployments. The pause could also affect local cloud providers that depend on Nvidia hardware to power their services.

Industry response

Industry analysts note that the pause is part of a growing trend of chipmakers adjusting supply chains in response to geopolitical pressures. While the immediate effect is a slowdown in hardware availability, the long‑term shift may accelerate the development of regional chip production capabilities.
