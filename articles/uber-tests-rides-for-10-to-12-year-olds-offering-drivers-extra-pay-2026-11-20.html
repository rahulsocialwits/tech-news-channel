Uber Tests Rides for 10‑ to 12‑Year‑Olds, Offering Drivers Extra Pay

2026-11-20 · 3 min read
Uber is piloting a new service that lets parents book rides for children aged 10 to 12, with drivers receiving higher compensation for the trips.

Uber is expanding its ride‑hailing platform to include a new service aimed at families with older children. The company is testing a feature that allows parents to book rides for kids between the ages of 10 and 12, a move that could reshape how parents manage transportation for their teens.

How the Service Works

Under the pilot, parents can request a ride for their child through the Uber app, just as they would for themselves. The request is routed to nearby drivers who are willing to accept the trip. Once a driver picks up the child, the app automatically adds a surcharge to the fare, ensuring that drivers receive higher compensation for the additional responsibility.

Why Uber Is Targeting This Age Group

Uber’s decision to focus on 10‑ to 12‑year‑olds comes after a broader industry trend toward family‑friendly transportation solutions. The company’s leadership believes that providing a safe, reliable option for older children will reduce the need for parents to drive themselves or rely on school buses.

Driver Incentives and Safety Measures

Drivers participating in the pilot receive a premium pay rate that reflects the extra time and care required to transport minors. Uber has also introduced a set of safety protocols, including mandatory driver background checks and in‑app child‑seat verification tools, to ensure that the service meets regulatory standards.

Next Steps for Uber Kids

Uber plans to roll out the service in select cities over the next few months, gathering data on usage patterns and driver feedback. If the pilot proves successful, the company may expand the age range and add features such as in‑vehicle monitoring and real‑time parent notifications.
