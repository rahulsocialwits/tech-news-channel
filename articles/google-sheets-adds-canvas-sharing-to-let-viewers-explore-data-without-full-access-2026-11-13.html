Google Sheets Adds Canvas Sharing to Let Viewers Explore Data Without Full Access

2026-11-13 · 3 min read
Google Sheets now offers a canvas sharing feature that lets collaborators view and interact with data visualizations while keeping the underlying spreadsheet private.

Google has expanded the sharing options for its spreadsheet tool, introducing a canvas mode that lets users share interactive views of their data without exposing the full spreadsheet. The new feature, announced on November 13, 2026, allows a sheet owner to create a canvas that contains charts, tables, and other visual elements. Viewers can explore the data through these visualizations, but they cannot edit the underlying cells or see the raw data that feeds the charts.

How Canvas Sharing Works

When a user selects the canvas option, Google Sheets generates a read‑only view that includes only the elements the owner chooses to display. The canvas can be shared via a link or embedded in other documents, and viewers can interact with the visual components—such as filtering a chart or hovering over data points—without gaining edit rights or access to the spreadsheet’s source data.

Benefits for Data Privacy and Collaboration

Organizations that need to share insights with stakeholders while protecting sensitive information can now do so more securely. The canvas mode reduces the risk of accidental data exposure and limits the ability of external parties to manipulate the underlying data set.

Getting Started

To create a canvas, open a Google Sheet, click the Share button, and choose “Create canvas.” From there, you can select which charts, tables, or other visual elements to include. Once the canvas is ready, you can copy the shareable link or embed it in a website or Google Slides presentation.
