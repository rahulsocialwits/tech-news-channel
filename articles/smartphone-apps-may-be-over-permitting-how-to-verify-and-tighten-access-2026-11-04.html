Smartphone Apps May Be Over‑Permitting: How to Verify and Tighten Access

2026-11-04 · 3 min read
Users can now check which permissions their phone apps have and revoke unnecessary ones, protecting privacy and battery life.

Recent research shows that many mobile applications request more permissions than they actually need to function. While most permissions are essential for core features, a handful of apps have been found to ask for access that goes beyond their stated purpose. This can expose users to privacy risks and drain device resources.

Why the Over‑Permission Problem Matters

When an app requests a broad set of permissions—such as location, contacts, or microphone—it can potentially collect data that the user never intended to share. In addition, unnecessary permissions can lead to higher battery consumption and increased data usage. For developers, over‑permissioning can erode user trust and lead to negative reviews.

How to Check App Permissions on Android

Android users can quickly audit permissions by navigating to Settings → Apps → [App Name] → Permissions. Here, each permission is listed with a toggle that can be turned off if the app does not require it. For example, a simple flashlight app should not need access to contacts or camera.

Best Practices for Developers

App creators should adopt the principle of least privilege: request only the permissions that are absolutely necessary for the app’s core functionality. This not only improves security but also enhances the user experience by reducing background activity.

What Users Should Do Next

1. Review the permissions for each installed app. 2. Disable any that are not essential. 3. Keep the operating system and apps updated, as newer versions often include improved permission controls. 4. Consider using third‑party privacy tools that provide additional oversight.
