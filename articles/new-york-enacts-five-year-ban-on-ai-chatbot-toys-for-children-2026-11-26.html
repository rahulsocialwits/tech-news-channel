New York Enacts Five‑Year Ban on AI Chatbot Toys for Children

2026-11-26 · 3 min read
New York Governor Kathy Hochul signs law prohibiting sale of AI chatbot toys to kids for five years.

In a move aimed at protecting children from potential risks associated with artificial intelligence, New York Governor Kathy Hochul signed a law that bans the sale of AI chatbot toys for a period of five years. The legislation, which took effect on Thursday, targets products that use conversational AI to interact with children, citing concerns over privacy, data collection, and the lack of robust safety safeguards.

Key Provisions of the Ban

The law prohibits the sale, distribution, and marketing of any toy that incorporates an AI chatbot capable of engaging in natural language conversations with children. It also requires manufacturers to provide clear disclosures about data usage and to implement child‑safe settings. Violations can result in civil penalties and potential criminal charges.

Industry Reactions

Tech companies that produce AI‑powered toys have expressed concern that the ban could stifle innovation in the rapidly growing market for interactive learning devices. Some industry groups argue that existing consumer protection regulations already address many of the risks, while others support the state's proactive stance on child safety.

Broader Context

The legislation follows a growing trend of governments worldwide reevaluating the role of AI in consumer products, especially those aimed at younger audiences. Similar discussions have emerged in the European Union and the United Kingdom, where regulators are exploring stricter oversight of AI applications in education and entertainment.
