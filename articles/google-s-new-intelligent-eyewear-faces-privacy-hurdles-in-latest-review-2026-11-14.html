Google’s New Intelligent Eyewear Faces Privacy Hurdles in Latest Review

2026-11-14 · 3 min read
A recent review of Google’s Intelligent Eyewear highlights performance strengths and privacy concerns that could shape the smart glasses market.

Google’s latest entry into the smart‑glasses arena, the Intelligent Eyewear, has been put through its paces by tech journalist Abner in the Pixelated episode 125. The review, which aired on November 13, 2026, offers a balanced look at the device’s capabilities and the privacy challenges it inherits from its parent company’s broader ecosystem.

What the glasses can do

The eyewear runs on a custom version of Android and integrates seamlessly with Google Assistant, allowing users to issue voice commands, view notifications, and access navigation overlays. The display is a lightweight, transparent panel that projects information directly onto the wearer’s field of view, a feature that reviewers praised for its minimal intrusion into everyday sight.

Privacy concerns surface

While the device’s functionality is impressive, the review raises questions about data collection. Google’s history of gathering user data for advertising and service improvement means that the glasses could potentially record audio, video, and biometric information. The reviewer notes that the company has not yet clarified how this data will be stored, shared, or protected, leaving consumers uncertain about the extent of surveillance.

Industry context

Google’s attempt to enter the smart‑glasses market follows Meta’s earlier foray, which faced similar scrutiny. The Pixelated review suggests that Google’s approach may offer a more privacy‑friendly alternative, but only time will tell if the company can address the lingering concerns that have plagued wearable tech.
