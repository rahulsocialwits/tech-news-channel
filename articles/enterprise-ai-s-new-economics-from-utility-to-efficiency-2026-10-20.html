Enterprise AI’s New Economics: From Utility to Efficiency

2026-10-20 · 3 min read
How companies are shifting from simply adopting AI to optimizing its use for maximum value.

In a recent piece from MIT Technology Review, the author draws a powerful analogy between the early days of electricity and the current state of enterprise AI. Just as the first generators powered a handful of machines, early AI pilots ran on a limited set of use cases, often without a clear focus on efficiency or scalability.

From Utility to Infrastructure

The article explains that once electricity became a ubiquitous infrastructure, every process in a factory or office began to depend on it. The question shifted from “Can we use it?” to “How efficiently are we using it?” The same transition is happening with AI in businesses today. AI is no longer a niche tool; it is becoming a foundational layer that underpins a wide range of operations, from customer service to supply chain optimization.

Efficiency as the New Value Driver

Enterprise leaders are now looking beyond the initial ROI of AI projects. They are asking how to reduce the cost of running AI models, how to streamline data pipelines, and how to ensure that AI systems can be maintained and scaled without prohibitive overhead. The article highlights that companies that focus on these efficiency metrics are better positioned to extract sustained value from AI investments.

Key Takeaways for Businesses

  • Infrastructure Investment: Building robust data and compute infrastructure is essential for long‑term AI success.
  • Operational Efficiency: Reducing model training times, optimizing inference pipelines, and automating model governance can lower costs.
  • Strategic Alignment: AI initiatives should be closely tied to core business objectives to maximize impact.
  • Continuous Improvement: Regularly revisiting and refining AI workflows ensures that efficiency gains are sustained.

As AI moves from a novelty to a core business capability, the focus is shifting from “can we do it?” to “how well can we do it?” Companies that adopt this mindset will likely lead the next wave of digital transformation.
