US Commerce Department Expands AI Chip Export Controls to 24 Countries

2026-11-19 · 3 min read
The new rule bars data‑center operators outside the U.S. from using advanced American AI chips to serve clients in 24 nations, including China, Iran and Russia.

The U.S. Commerce Department announced an interim final rule on November 16 that extends its export‑control regime to cover advanced American artificial‑intelligence chips used in data‑center operations abroad. The regulation prohibits any data‑center operator located outside the United States from deploying these chips to provide cloud services to businesses in 24 countries, including China, Iran and Russia.

Scope of the Rule

Under the new rule, the focus is on the supply chain for high‑performance AI hardware. Operators who rely on U.S. chips for machine‑learning workloads will need to obtain a license before they can continue offering services to the affected jurisdictions. The measure is part of a broader U.S. strategy to limit the flow of advanced technology that could enhance adversarial capabilities.

Industry Impact

Cloud providers with global footprints are expected to reassess their hardware sourcing and service offerings. Companies that have built their infrastructure around U.S. chip manufacturers will face additional compliance costs and potential disruptions if they cannot secure the necessary licenses. The rule also signals a tightening of the technology export landscape, prompting firms to explore alternative chip suppliers or to shift workloads to compliant regions.

Response from Stakeholders

Industry groups have called for clearer guidance on how the rule will be enforced and what constitutes a “data‑center operator.” Some analysts warn that the restriction could slow the adoption of AI services in emerging markets, while others argue it is a necessary step to safeguard national security.
