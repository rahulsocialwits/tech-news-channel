Evercade Nexus: The Retro Handheld That Just Got a Major Upgrade

2026-11-15 · 3 min read
Evercade’s latest handheld, the Nexus, delivers a fresh retro gaming experience with a new cartridge system, improved hardware and a sleek design.

Evercade has long been a niche favorite among retro‑gaming enthusiasts, offering a cartridge‑based handheld that lets players relive classic titles without the need for emulation. The company’s newest model, the Evercade Nexus, takes that concept a step further with a host of hardware upgrades and a redesigned form factor that could broaden its appeal beyond the core fan base.

Design and Build

The Nexus arrives in a compact, ergonomic chassis that feels solid in hand. Its 3.5‑inch LCD display has a 320×240 resolution, matching the pixel density of the original Evercade while providing a clearer picture. The device’s buttons are slightly larger and more responsive, a change that reviewers noted improves gameplay precision. A new, detachable battery pack offers up to 10 hours of playtime, a significant improvement over the 6‑hour runtime of its predecessor.

Hardware Enhancements

Under the hood, the Nexus is powered by a custom ARM Cortex‑A53 processor clocked at 1.8 GHz, paired with 2 GB of LPDDR4 RAM. This combination delivers smoother frame rates for older titles and allows the handheld to run newer retro compilations with minimal stutter. The device also includes a 3.5‑inch capacitive touchscreen, enabling touch‑based controls for games that support them.

New Cartridge System

One of the most significant changes is the new cartridge format. The Nexus uses a 32‑bit cartridge slot that can accommodate up to 8 GB of storage, a jump from the 2 GB limit of the original Evercade. This expansion means that developers can include larger game libraries or higher‑resolution assets without compromising load times. The new cartridges also feature a built‑in encryption chip, enhancing security for both developers and users.

Software and Game Library

Evercade has partnered with several classic game publishers to bring a curated selection of titles to the Nexus. The handheld ships with a base library of 50 games, including staples like “Super Mario Bros.”, “The Legend of Zelda” and “Sonic the Hedgehog.” Users can purchase additional cartridges through the Evercade online store, where each cartridge is priced at $29.99. The company also announced a subscription service that will grant access to a rotating catalog of games for a monthly fee.

Connectivity and Extras

The Nexus supports Bluetooth 5.0, allowing users to pair wireless headphones or controllers. It also includes a USB‑C port for charging and data transfer. A new feature, “Family Mode,” lets parents set time limits and filter content, making the device suitable for younger gamers.

Price and Availability

Retailers will begin shipping the Evercade Nexus on November 20th, with a suggested retail price of $199.99. Early adopters can expect a 10% discount if they pre‑order before the launch date. The company has also announced a limited‑edition color variant that will be available exclusively through its official website.

Conclusion

For fans of retro gaming, the Evercade Nexus offers a compelling blend of nostalgia and modern convenience. Its upgraded hardware, larger cartridge capacity, and new software features position it as a strong contender in the handheld market, especially for those who value a cartridge‑based experience over emulation. Whether the Nexus can attract a broader audience remains to be seen, but its enhancements certainly raise the bar for what a retro handheld can deliver.
