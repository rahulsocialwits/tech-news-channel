Tesla’s New Roadster Takes to the Skies with SpaceX‑Powered Thrusters

2026-10-16 · 3 min read
Tesla unveiled a next‑generation Roadster that can launch itself into the air using SpaceX‑derived thrusters, sparking excitement among automotive and aerospace enthusiasts.

At a high‑profile event in Texas, Tesla announced the Roadster 2, a luxury sports car that can actually fly. The new model is equipped with a set of cold‑gas thrusters that allow it to lift off the ground, perform aerial maneuvers, and then safely land back on the road. The demonstration showcased the car leaping over a Cybercab, a Cybertruck, a Tesla Semi, a Model Y, and a Model 3, highlighting the vehicle’s impressive thrust and control capabilities.

SpaceX‑Powered Technology

The thrusters are based on technology developed by SpaceX, the company that launched the Falcon 9 rockets. By integrating SpaceX’s propulsion system, Tesla has turned a conventional roadster into a hybrid ground‑air vehicle. The system uses a cold‑gas engine that expels compressed gas to generate lift, allowing the car to reach altitudes of several meters before descending.

Design and Performance

While the Roadster 2 retains the sleek, aerodynamic shape of its predecessor, the addition of thrusters required significant engineering adjustments. Tesla’s design team added a lightweight chassis and reinforced the body to handle the stresses of vertical take‑off and landing. The car’s battery pack remains a high‑capacity lithium‑ion system, providing the power needed for both road and air travel.

Pricing and Availability

According to Engadget, the Roadster 2 is available for reservation at a starting price of $250,000. The high cost reflects the advanced propulsion system and the limited production run. Tesla has not yet announced a specific delivery window, but the company plans to begin production later this year.

Industry Impact

The Roadster 2 represents a bold step toward integrating aerospace technology into consumer vehicles. If successful, it could open new possibilities for rapid transit, emergency response, and recreational aviation. Tesla’s move also signals a growing trend of automotive manufacturers exploring vertical lift solutions, potentially reshaping the future of personal transportation.
