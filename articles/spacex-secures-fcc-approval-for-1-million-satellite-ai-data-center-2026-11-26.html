SpaceX Secures FCC Approval for 1‑Million‑Satellite AI Data Center

2026-11-26 · 3 min read
SpaceX receives regulatory green light to launch a million satellites aimed at powering an orbital AI data center, marking a major step toward space‑based cloud computing.

SpaceX has been granted approval by the Federal Communications Commission to deploy a constellation of one million satellites, a move that will underpin the company’s planned AI data center in orbit. The FCC’s decision clears the way for the launch of the massive network, which is intended to provide high‑speed, low‑latency connectivity for data‑intensive AI workloads.

Why a million satellites?

The sheer scale of the constellation is designed to support the bandwidth and redundancy required for large‑scale machine‑learning models. By distributing computing resources across the globe, SpaceX aims to reduce the distance data must travel, thereby cutting latency for real‑time AI applications.

Implications for cloud services

With an orbital data center, SpaceX could offer a new tier of cloud services that combine the reliability of terrestrial infrastructure with the expansive coverage of space. This development could shift the competitive landscape for cloud providers, especially those focused on AI workloads that demand rapid data access.

Next steps

SpaceX will need to coordinate launch schedules, satellite manufacturing, and ground‑segment infrastructure to bring the constellation online. The company has already begun testing prototypes and is working with partners to integrate the satellites into its existing Starlink network.
