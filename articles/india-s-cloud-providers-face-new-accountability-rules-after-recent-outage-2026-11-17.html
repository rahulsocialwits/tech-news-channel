India’s Cloud Providers Face New Accountability Rules After Recent Outage

2026-11-17 · 3 min read
India’s data‑center outage prompts lawmakers to tighten reporting requirements for cloud services.

India’s cloud sector is under new scrutiny after a recent outage that left Microsoft 365 users unable to access their productivity suite. The incident has sparked a debate over whether Indian law requires cloud providers to report such disruptions promptly and what obligations they have to customers during service interruptions.

Legal backdrop for cloud outages

Under current Indian regulations, there is no explicit mandate for cloud companies to disclose outages in real time. The recent Microsoft incident highlighted gaps in the existing framework, prompting lawmakers to consider tighter reporting rules that would align India with international best practices.

Comparative accountability

In other jurisdictions, cloud operators are often required to notify customers within hours of a service disruption and to provide detailed post‑incident reports. The lack of similar requirements in India has raised concerns among businesses that rely on cloud services for critical operations.

Impact on Indian customers

Customers affected by the outage have limited recourse under current law. The proposed changes aim to give them clearer information about the cause, duration, and mitigation steps taken by providers, potentially improving trust and transparency in the market.

Industry response

Major cloud players, including Microsoft, have acknowledged the incident and expressed a commitment to enhancing incident communication. The industry is also exploring internal best practices to reduce the likelihood of future outages.
