French Lawmakers Approve €0.055‑Per‑GB Internet Tax, Targeting Major Tech Firms

2026-10-31 · 3 min read
France’s new internet tax will charge users €0.055 per gigabyte of digital content, potentially generating €10 billion annually and affecting Google, Meta, Netflix and TikTok.

Paris has approved a new digital content tax that will charge French internet subscribers €0.055 for every gigabyte of data they download. The measure is aimed at the biggest players in the online ecosystem, including Google, Meta, Netflix and possibly TikTok, and is expected to bring in roughly €10 billion a year.

How the Tax Works

The tax applies to content delivered to subscribers, not to the infrastructure that carries it. Users will see the charge on their monthly bills, while providers will need to report usage to the government. The law is still under parliamentary review, but the initial proposal has already sparked debate among tech giants and consumer groups.

Industry Reactions

Major tech firms have expressed concern that the tax could stifle innovation and increase costs for consumers. Some have suggested that the measure might be a step toward a broader European digital services tax. Meanwhile, French regulators argue that the levy is a fair way to redistribute the benefits of digital services to the public sector.

What It Means for Consumers

For everyday users, the tax will be reflected in higher data charges, especially for heavy streamers and gamers. The government claims the revenue will support public services, but critics warn that it could lead to higher prices for all digital content.
