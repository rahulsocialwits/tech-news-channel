HyperX Unveils First Open‑Back Gaming Headset: The Cloud Alpha Air

2026-10-18 · 3 min read
HyperX’s new Cloud Alpha Air headset offers an open‑back design, lightweight comfort and solid audio, but falls short on mic pickup and software support.

HyperX has stepped into the open‑back headset arena with the Cloud Alpha Air, its first model to ditch the traditional closed‑back design. The company markets the headset as a budget‑friendly option that still delivers the high‑quality sound HyperX is known for. The launch comes at a time when gamers are looking for lighter, more breathable gear for long sessions, and the Cloud Alpha Air aims to fill that niche.

Design and Comfort

The headset’s most noticeable feature is its open‑back construction. Unlike the closed‑back design that traps heat and can feel claustrophobic, the Cloud Alpha Air lets air circulate around the ears. This design choice, combined with a lightweight build, makes the headset comfortable for extended play. The ear cups are padded with a soft material that feels gentle on the skin, and the adjustable headband distributes weight evenly.

Audio Performance

HyperX claims the Cloud Alpha Air delivers “very good” sound quality. Reviewers have noted that the headset produces clear highs and a balanced midrange, with a decent bass response for a budget model. The open‑back design does introduce some ambient noise, but for many users the trade‑off is worth the increased comfort.

Microphone and Software Limitations

While the headset shines in audio, it falls short in other areas. The built‑in microphone has low pickup sensitivity, which can make voice communication in games less reliable. Additionally, the headset does not currently integrate with HyperX’s peripheral software, limiting users who want to tweak settings or use advanced features.

Price and Availability

The Cloud Alpha Air is positioned as an affordable option, with a price point that sits below many premium open‑back headsets. It is available for purchase through major electronics retailers and online stores. HyperX’s marketing suggests the headset is ideal for casual gamers and those who prioritize comfort over the highest audio fidelity.

Conclusion

HyperX’s Cloud Alpha Air marks a significant step for the brand, expanding its lineup into the open‑back market. The headset offers a lightweight, comfortable experience with solid audio for its price. However, the limited microphone performance and lack of software support may deter users who need more advanced features. For gamers seeking a budget-friendly, breathable headset, the Cloud Alpha Air is worth considering.
