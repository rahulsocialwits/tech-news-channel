China Startup Langdao Silex Demonstrates 15‑W EUV Light Source Prototype

2026-10-17 · 3 min read
Langdao Silex showcased a 15‑W laser‑produced plasma EUV source at a trade show, a step toward future EUV lithography tools.

At the recent International Semiconductor Conference, Chinese startup Langdao Silex unveiled a prototype extreme ultraviolet (EUV) light source that delivers 15 watts of output power. The device uses a laser‑produced plasma (LPP) approach, a technology that has been under development for several years but has rarely reached practical power levels for lithography applications.

Key Technical Highlights

The LPP system operates by firing a high‑energy laser pulse onto a tin droplet, creating a plasma that emits EUV radiation at a wavelength of 13.5 nanometers. Langdao’s prototype achieves a 90‑percent domestic component composition, a notable milestone given that most EUV systems rely heavily on imported parts.

While the 15‑W output is modest compared to the 200‑W sources used in current commercial lithography tools, the demonstration marks a significant proof of concept. Engineers noted that scaling the power while maintaining beam quality and stability remains a major engineering challenge.

Implications for the Semiconductor Industry

EU lithography is the backbone of advanced chip manufacturing, enabling the production of processors with 5‑nanometer and smaller nodes. The high cost and complexity of EUV tools have kept the technology largely within the purview of a handful of global foundries. A domestically produced, lower‑cost EUV source could democratize access to next‑generation lithography, potentially reducing reliance on foreign suppliers.

However, experts caution that even if the power output can be increased, additional components—such as high‑precision mirrors, vacuum chambers, and radiation shielding—must also be developed to create a fully functional lithography tool. The current prototype is a critical first step, but it is still years away from commercial viability.

Future Outlook

Langdao Silex plans to refine the LPP design, aiming to boost power output and improve beam stability. The company also intends to collaborate with semiconductor fabs to test the system in a production environment. If successful, the technology could accelerate the deployment of EUV lithography in China and other emerging markets.

For now, the 15‑W prototype serves as a promising proof of concept that laser‑produced plasma EUV sources can be engineered with a high proportion of domestic components, a key consideration for countries seeking to reduce supply‑chain dependencies in critical technology sectors.
