PsiQuantum Unveils 8×8 Optical Switch to Supercharge AI Data Center GPU Networks

2026-10-30 · 3 min read
PsiQuantum’s new optical switch prototype offers 1.2 dB loss, far below the 3 dB limit in data centers, promising faster AI workloads.

In a breakthrough that could reshape the backbone of AI‑driven workloads, PsiQuantum has demonstrated an eight‑port optical switch that loses just 1.2 decibels of light. The prototype, built in a lab setting, represents a significant improvement over the 3 dB loss ceiling that most data center networks tolerate.

Why Loss Matters in AI Workloads

Artificial intelligence models increasingly rely on massive GPU clusters that must exchange terabytes of data every second. Optical switches are a key component in these networks, routing light‑based signals between GPUs. However, each switch introduces signal loss, and the cumulative effect can degrade performance or force costly redundancy.

PsiQuantum’s switch, with its 1.2 dB loss, sits well below the industry’s 3 dB threshold. This means fewer repeaters are needed, lower power consumption, and potentially higher data throughput—all critical for scaling AI training and inference.

From Photonics to Practicality

The company’s team leveraged its expertise in photonic quantum computing to engineer the switch. While the prototype is currently an 8×8 device, PsiQuantum plans to scale the architecture to larger arrays. The goal is to create a modular, high‑performance switch that can be integrated into existing data center fabrics without major redesigns.

Implications for Cloud Providers

Cloud giants that host AI services stand to benefit from reduced latency and energy costs. By integrating PsiQuantum’s low‑loss switches, providers could deliver faster model training times and lower operational expenses. The technology also aligns with the growing demand for greener data centers, as optical solutions consume less power than their electronic counterparts.

Next Steps and Market Outlook

PsiQuantum is currently refining the prototype for larger scale production. The company has indicated plans to collaborate with major GPU manufacturers and data center operators to pilot the technology in real‑world environments. If successful, the switch could become a standard component in next‑generation AI infrastructure.
