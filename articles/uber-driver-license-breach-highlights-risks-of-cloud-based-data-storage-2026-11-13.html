Uber Driver License Breach Highlights Risks of Cloud‑Based Data Storage

2026-11-13 · 3 min read
Uber confirmed that hackers accessed thousands of driver license records stored by Fireworks AI, underscoring the need for stronger cloud security measures.

Uber has confirmed that a data breach exposed the driver license information of thousands of its drivers. The stolen records were stored in the cloud by Fireworks AI, a company that provides AI‑powered services to Uber and other clients.

What Happened?

According to Uber’s statement, the breach occurred when unauthorized actors accessed Fireworks AI’s cloud infrastructure. The attackers were able to retrieve driver license numbers, dates of birth and other identifying details. Uber has not disclosed the exact number of affected drivers, but the company said the data set was “substantial.”

Why It Matters

Driver license data is highly sensitive. If it falls into the wrong hands, it can be used for identity theft, fraud or targeted phishing attacks. The incident also raises questions about how ride‑sharing companies manage data that is stored off‑premises.

Industry Response

Fireworks AI has issued a statement saying it is cooperating with Uber and law enforcement to investigate the breach. The company also announced plans to review its security protocols and implement additional safeguards.

Lessons for Cloud Users

Security teams should ensure that data stored in third‑party cloud services is encrypted at rest and in transit, and that access controls are strictly enforced. Regular penetration testing and continuous monitoring can help detect suspicious activity before it leads to a breach.
