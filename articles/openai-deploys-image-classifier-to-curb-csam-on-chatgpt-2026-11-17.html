OpenAI Deploys Image Classifier to Curb CSAM on ChatGPT

2026-11-17 · 3 min read
OpenAI has added a new image‑classification system and warning screens to its ChatGPT platform to detect and block child sexual abuse material.

OpenAI announced today that it has rolled out a new image‑classification system designed to identify child sexual abuse material (CSAM) in user‑submitted images on ChatGPT. The update also introduces warning screens that appear when the system flags potentially disallowed content, giving users a chance to review the material before it is processed further.

How the System Works

The classifier uses a combination of machine‑learning models trained on a curated dataset of known CSAM. When a user uploads an image, the system scans it in real time and assigns a risk score. If the score exceeds a predefined threshold, the image is blocked and a warning screen is displayed. The user can then choose to confirm that the image is safe or to delete it.

Implications for Safety and Compliance

OpenAI’s move comes amid growing regulatory pressure to prevent the spread of illegal content on AI platforms. By adding a proactive detection layer, the company aims to reduce the likelihood that CSAM is inadvertently shared or used to train future models. The warning screen also provides a transparent user experience, allowing individuals to understand why a particular image was flagged.

Industry Context

Other tech firms have taken similar steps. Apple, for example, recently introduced disk‑access controls to limit AI agents from running amok on Macs. These measures reflect a broader industry trend toward embedding safety mechanisms directly into AI systems.

Next Steps for OpenAI

OpenAI plans to refine the classifier over the coming months, incorporating user feedback and expanding the dataset to improve accuracy. The company also intends to share its approach with the wider AI community to encourage best practices in content moderation.
