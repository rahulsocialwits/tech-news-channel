Intel Advances U.S. Chip Production with New Fab 62 Tools

2026-10-22 · 3 min read
Intel has installed advanced production equipment at its Arizona Fab 62, marking a key step toward expanding U.S. semiconductor manufacturing capacity.

Intel Corp. announced today that it has installed a suite of production tools at its Fab 62 facility in Arizona, a move that signals progress toward the company’s goal of expanding advanced semiconductor manufacturing in the United States. The installation is part of Intel’s broader strategy to increase domestic chip output and reduce reliance on overseas fabs.

Fab 62 Tooling Upgrade

The new equipment, which includes state‑of‑the‑art lithography and etching systems, is designed to support the production of high‑volume, high‑performance chips. Intel’s engineering team reported that the tools will enable the facility to produce advanced nodes that are critical for next‑generation processors and other high‑performance applications.

Strategic Expansion in the U.S.

Intel’s investment in Fab 62 follows recent updates on its Ohio chip fab, where the company confirmed plans to use high‑NA EUV lithography for high‑volume manufacturing by 2028. Together, the Arizona and Ohio facilities represent a significant expansion of Intel’s domestic manufacturing footprint.

Implications for the U.S. Tech Ecosystem

By bolstering its U.S. production capacity, Intel aims to strengthen the country’s semiconductor supply chain and support the growing demand for chips in automotive, data center, and consumer electronics markets. The company’s focus on advanced manufacturing technologies is expected to drive innovation and create high‑skill jobs in the region.

Next Steps

Intel plans to begin production at Fab 62 later this year, with full operational capacity expected in the coming months. The company will continue to invest in research and development to maintain its competitive edge in the rapidly evolving semiconductor industry.
