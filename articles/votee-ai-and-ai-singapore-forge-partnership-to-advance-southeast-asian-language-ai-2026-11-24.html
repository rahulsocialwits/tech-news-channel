Votee AI and AI Singapore Forge Partnership to Advance Southeast Asian Language AI

2026-11-24 · 3 min read
Votee AI and AI Singapore sign a memorandum of understanding to collaborate on AI research for Southeast Asian languages, aiming to improve language models and digital inclusion.

In a move that could reshape the AI landscape in Southeast Asia, Hong Kong‑based Votee AI and Singapore’s AI Singapore (AISG) have signed a memorandum of understanding (MoU) to explore joint research on artificial intelligence for Southeast Asian languages. The agreement, announced on 23 November 2026, signals a growing recognition that mainstream AI models often under‑represent the linguistic diversity of the region.

Why Southeast Asian languages matter for AI

Most large language models are trained on English‑centric data sets, which limits their effectiveness in non‑English speaking markets. Southeast Asia is home to more than 600 million people who speak a wide array of languages, many of which lack digital resources. By focusing on these languages, Votee AI and AISG aim to bridge the gap between global AI capabilities and local user needs.

Votee AI’s expertise

Votee AI, a startup that has built a platform for multilingual AI training, has already demonstrated success in developing models for languages such as Thai, Vietnamese, and Bahasa Indonesia. The company’s technology leverages transfer learning to adapt existing models to low‑resource languages, reducing the need for massive data sets.

AI Singapore’s role

AI Singapore, a national AI research institute, has a mandate to accelerate AI adoption across Singapore’s industries. The MoU will allow AISG to provide access to its research facilities, datasets, and funding opportunities, while Votee AI brings its specialized expertise in multilingual model development.

Potential impact on the region

By combining resources, the partnership could produce more accurate speech recognition, natural language understanding, and translation tools tailored to local dialects. This, in turn, could enhance digital services such as e‑government portals, e‑commerce platforms, and educational apps, making them more accessible to a broader audience.

Future collaboration plans

The MoU outlines several joint initiatives, including shared research projects, co‑hosting workshops for developers, and joint publications in academic journals. Both parties also plan to explore funding opportunities from regional governments and international donors to support the development of open‑source language resources.

Industry reaction

Industry analysts see the partnership as a strategic step toward democratizing AI. “Southeast Asia’s linguistic diversity has been a blind spot for many AI companies,” said a spokesperson for a leading AI research firm. “Collaborations like this can accelerate the creation of inclusive AI solutions that serve millions of users.”
