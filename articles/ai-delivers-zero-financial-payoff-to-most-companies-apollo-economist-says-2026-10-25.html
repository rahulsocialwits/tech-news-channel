AI Delivers Zero Financial Payoff to Most Companies, Apollo Economist Says

2026-10-25 · 3 min read
Apollo economist reports that AI investments have yielded no financial returns for non‑tech firms, highlighting a stark divide in corporate earnings.

In a recent analysis, Apollo economist David G. Smith revealed that artificial intelligence has delivered no measurable financial payoff to the majority of corporate America outside the technology sector. The study, published on October 25, 2026, examined the return on investment for AI initiatives across the S&P 500 and found that only the largest tech firms—often referred to as the Magnificent Seven—have seen a significant lift in revenue per employee.

Key Findings

Smith’s report highlights a growing divide: while tech giants continue to cash in on AI, the rest of the corporate landscape remains largely untouched by the technology’s economic benefits. The analysis points to rising token costs and the high capital intensity of AI projects as potential barriers for non‑tech companies.

Implications for Corporate Strategy

For businesses outside the tech bubble, the findings suggest a need to reassess AI adoption strategies. Companies may need to focus on more cost‑effective, niche applications of AI rather than large‑scale, high‑budget deployments that have proven unprofitable in the broader market.
