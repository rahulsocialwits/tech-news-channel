Meta’s Muse Turns AI Agent Into Your Personal Call Concierge

2026-11-13 · 3 min read
Meta’s new Muse feature lets paid subscribers schedule appointments, make reservations and even wait on hold for you, turning the AI assistant into a hands‑free call handler.

Meta has expanded the capabilities of its AI assistant, Muse, to include a new call‑handling mode that can schedule appointments, make reservations and even wait on hold for users. The feature is available only to subscribers of the paid Muse plan, which is part of Meta’s broader effort to monetize its AI offerings.

How Muse Now Handles Calls

When a user activates Muse’s call mode, the assistant can place a phone call on the user’s behalf. It can read the user’s calendar to find an appropriate time slot, dial the target number, and then manage the conversation. If the user needs to wait on hold, Muse will stay on the line, listening to the music or announcements and then return to the user when the call is answered.

Practical Use Cases

Typical scenarios include booking a dentist appointment, reserving a table at a restaurant, or calling a service provider. The assistant can also handle simple follow‑up tasks, such as confirming a reservation or sending a text message after the call.

Subscription Requirements

Only users who have subscribed to Muse’s paid tier can access the call feature. Meta has not yet disclosed the pricing for the subscription, but the company has indicated that the feature is part of a broader suite of premium services that will be rolled out over the coming months.

Privacy and Security Considerations

Meta has stated that all calls made through Muse are encrypted and that the assistant does not record or store the conversation. Users can also opt out of the feature at any time. The company has emphasized that the feature is designed to protect user privacy while providing convenience.

Future Outlook

Meta’s move to enable AI agents to handle phone calls reflects a growing trend in the industry to integrate AI into everyday communication tasks. If the feature proves popular, it could pave the way for more advanced voice‑controlled services, such as automated customer support or personal concierge services.
