Google, OpenAI, Anthropic and Microsoft Unite on New Scraping Controls for AI Training

2026-10-31 · 3 min read
Major AI firms announce a shared signal to block web scraping for training models, aiming to protect content owners and improve transparency.

In a landmark move, Google, OpenAI, Anthropic and Microsoft have pledged to support a new common signal that will allow website owners to block automated scraping for AI training. The initiative, announced on October 31, 2026, builds on the existing robots.txt protocol by adding a dedicated tag that signals whether a site permits or disallows large‑scale data extraction for machine learning purposes.

What the new signal means for AI developers

AI companies that rely on web‑scraped data will need to update their crawling tools to recognize the new tag. Sites that opt in can provide curated datasets, while those that opt out will be automatically excluded from large‑scale scraping. This is expected to reduce the amount of unlicensed content used in training models and could streamline compliance with copyright laws.

Industry reaction

Google’s announcement highlighted the company’s commitment to “responsible AI” and its desire to balance innovation with respect for intellectual property. OpenAI and Anthropic echoed similar sentiments, noting that the signal will help them avoid legal pitfalls and improve the quality of their training data. Microsoft, which has been a long‑time partner in AI research, emphasized the importance of a unified standard across the industry.

Potential impact on the web ecosystem

Webmasters who wish to protect their content can now easily opt out of large‑scale scraping by adding the new tag to their robots.txt files. This could lead to a cleaner, more predictable environment for both content creators and AI developers. The move is also seen as a step toward greater transparency in how AI models are trained, potentially boosting public trust in the technology.
