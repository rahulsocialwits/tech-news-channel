GlobalFoundries Joins TSMC’s CoWoS Supply Chain with $2 B Silicon Interposer Deal

2026-10-09 · 3 min read
GlobalFoundries will produce silicon interposers for TSMC’s CoWoS in the U.S., a five‑year, $2 billion agreement that expands the U.S. supply chain for AI and HPC chips.

In a move that could reshape the U.S. semiconductor supply chain, GlobalFoundries announced a five‑year partnership with Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC) to produce silicon interposers for TSMC’s CoWoS (Chip‑on‑Wafer‑on‑Substrate) technology. The deal, valued at roughly $2 billion, will see GlobalFoundries build the interposers in the United States, allowing TSMC to keep its advanced AI and high‑performance computing (HPC) accelerators closer to U.S. customers without investing in cutting‑edge process nodes.

What Are Silicon Interposers and CoWoS?

Silicon interposers are thin, high‑density interconnect layers that enable multiple chips to be stacked and wired together on a single substrate. CoWoS is TSMC’s flagship 3D packaging technology that combines a silicon interposer with a wafer‑level package, delivering high bandwidth, low latency, and power efficiency—key attributes for next‑generation AI accelerators and HPC systems.

Why the U.S. Focus?

TSMC has long relied on overseas fabs for its most advanced nodes, but the partnership with GlobalFoundries allows it to keep the interposer fabrication in the U.S. This aligns with broader U.S. policy goals to reduce dependence on foreign supply chains for critical semiconductor components. By leveraging GlobalFoundries’ mature 300‑mm fabs, TSMC can continue to supply high‑performance chips to U.S. data centers and defense customers while mitigating geopolitical risks.

Impact on AI and HPC Markets

Both AI and HPC workloads demand dense, low‑latency interconnects. The CoWoS platform is already used in NVIDIA’s H100 Tensor Core GPUs and other AI accelerators. With GlobalFoundries producing the interposers domestically, manufacturers can reduce lead times and shipping costs, potentially accelerating the rollout of new AI models and supercomputing solutions.

Financial and Strategic Implications

The $2 billion agreement represents a significant investment in U.S. semiconductor manufacturing capacity. For GlobalFoundries, the partnership expands its portfolio beyond traditional logic and memory fabs, positioning it as a key player in the 3D packaging ecosystem. For TSMC, the deal strengthens its foothold in the U.S. market and demonstrates a commitment to building a resilient supply chain for high‑value components.

Future Outlook

As AI workloads continue to grow, the demand for advanced packaging solutions like CoWoS is expected to rise. The GlobalFoundries‑TSMC partnership could set a precedent for other fabless and foundry companies to collaborate on specialized manufacturing services within the United States. If successful, it may spur further investment in domestic semiconductor infrastructure and help the U.S. maintain a competitive edge in AI and HPC technologies.
