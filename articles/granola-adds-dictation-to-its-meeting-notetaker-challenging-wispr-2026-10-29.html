Granola Adds Dictation to Its Meeting Notetaker, Challenging Wispr

2026-10-29 · 3 min read
Granola expands its meeting‑note app with a new dictation feature, positioning itself against Wispr’s recent note‑taking enhancements.

Granola, the startup that has built a lightweight meeting‑notetaking app, announced today that it is adding a voice‑to‑text dictation feature. The move comes after Wispr, a competitor that recently introduced meeting‑notes functionality to its dictation platform, pushed the industry to rethink how voice data is captured and organized.

Why the timing matters

In the past year, the market for AI‑powered meeting assistants has exploded. Companies are racing to deliver real‑time transcription, keyword extraction, and actionable summaries. Granola’s decision to embed dictation directly into its notetaking workflow signals that it sees a growing demand for seamless voice capture that can be instantly transformed into searchable, shareable notes.

How Granola’s new feature works

Users can now start a meeting in Granola and simply speak into their device. The app records the audio, runs it through a speech‑recognition engine, and populates the note in real time. The transcription is automatically tagged with timestamps and speaker labels, allowing participants to jump to specific moments in the conversation. Granola also offers a “smart edit” mode that highlights key action items and decisions, giving teams a quick way to review what was agreed upon.

Competitive landscape

Wispr’s earlier launch of meeting‑notes functionality set a new standard for voice‑enabled collaboration tools. By adding dictation, Granola is closing the gap and positioning itself as a one‑stop solution for teams that want both a dedicated notetaking interface and robust voice capture. The feature also differentiates Granola from other note‑taking apps that rely on manual typing or third‑party transcription services.

Implications for users

For small and medium‑sized businesses, the integration means less friction during meetings. Teams no longer need to switch between a voice recorder and a note‑taking app; everything happens inside Granola. The real‑time transcription also helps participants who may have missed parts of the discussion, as they can review the text instantly. Additionally, the automatic tagging of speakers and timestamps makes it easier to reference specific points in post‑meeting follow‑ups.

Future prospects

Granola’s leadership has hinted at further AI enhancements, such as sentiment analysis and predictive task assignment, that could build on the new dictation capability. If the company can maintain a lightweight, privacy‑focused product while scaling its AI features, it could become a serious contender in the crowded market of meeting‑assistant tools.
