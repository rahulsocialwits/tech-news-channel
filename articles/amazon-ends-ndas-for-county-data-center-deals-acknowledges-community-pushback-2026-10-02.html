Amazon Ends NDAs for County Data Center Deals, Acknowledges Community Pushback

2026-10-02 · 3 min read
Amazon will no longer use non‑disclosure agreements with county officials for new data center sites, citing growing public concern and potential moratoriums.

Amazon has announced that it will stop using non‑disclosure agreements (NDAs) with county officials when negotiating new data center sites. The move comes after a wave of community backlash that has led to calls for moratoriums on data center construction across the United States.

Why the Change Matters

NDAs have long been a standard practice for tech companies seeking to keep details of their expansion plans confidential. However, the practice has drawn criticism from local governments and residents who argue that it limits transparency and hampers public input on projects that can have significant environmental and economic impacts.

Amazon’s Response

In a statement released on October 2, 2026, Amazon acknowledged that the use of NDAs had “created a perception of secrecy” and that the company is committed to engaging more openly with communities. The company said it would replace NDAs with more transparent agreements that still protect proprietary information but allow for public scrutiny of site selection criteria, environmental assessments, and community benefit plans.

Potential Impact on Data Center Development

Industry analysts suggest that Amazon’s decision could set a new standard for how cloud providers negotiate with local governments. By removing NDAs, Amazon may reduce the likelihood of community‑initiated moratoriums, which have stalled projects in states such as California and Oregon. The company’s move could also encourage other major cloud players to adopt similar practices, potentially accelerating the deployment of new data center infrastructure while maintaining a higher level of public trust.

Community and Environmental Considerations

Local officials have expressed relief that the company is addressing their concerns. “Transparency is key to building trust with residents,” said a spokesperson for a county in California that had previously threatened a moratorium. “We look forward to working with Amazon under the new agreement framework.”

Environmental groups, meanwhile, remain cautious. While the removal of NDAs is a positive step, they emphasize that the real measure of progress will be in the environmental safeguards Amazon implements, such as renewable energy sourcing and water‑efficient cooling systems.

Next Steps for Amazon

Amazon plans to roll out the new agreement model over the next six months, starting with its upcoming data center in Texas. The company will also publish a public dashboard detailing the criteria used for site selection, projected environmental impacts, and community benefit commitments.

As the cloud industry continues to expand, Amazon’s decision to forgo NDAs could signal a broader shift toward greater accountability and community engagement in data center development.
