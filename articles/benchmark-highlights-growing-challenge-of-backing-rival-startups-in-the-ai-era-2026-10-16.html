Benchmark Highlights Growing Challenge of Backing Rival Startups in the AI Era

2026-10-16 · 3 min read
Benchmark notes that the traditional venture rule against funding competing firms is eroding as AI startups proliferate, raising new partnership and conflict‑of‑interest questions for investors.

In a recent interview, Benchmark Capital’s partners acknowledged that the long‑standing venture‑capital rule against backing rival companies is becoming increasingly difficult to enforce in the fast‑moving artificial‑intelligence sector. The firm, which has backed some of Silicon Valley’s most successful AI ventures, said that the sheer volume of new AI startups and the overlapping nature of their technologies are blurring the lines that once kept investors from supporting direct competitors.

Why the Rule Is Under Pressure

Benchmark’s statement comes amid a surge of AI firms that are building similar models, data pipelines, and infrastructure. When a venture firm invests in one AI company, it often gains access to proprietary data, talent, and strategic insights that could be valuable to a rival. The traditional rule—often referred to as the “venture pact”—was designed to prevent such conflicts of interest and protect the interests of all portfolio companies.

Implications for Investors and Founders

For investors, the erosion of this rule means they must navigate a more complex landscape of potential conflicts. They may need to implement stricter internal governance, disclose more information to portfolio companies, or even reconsider the breadth of their investment mandates. Founders, on the other hand, could face increased scrutiny from potential investors who are wary of future conflicts that might arise if they later join a competitor.

Benchmark’s Approach Moving Forward

Benchmark has indicated that it will continue to evaluate each investment on a case‑by‑case basis, weighing the strategic fit against the potential for conflict. The firm also plans to engage more proactively with its portfolio companies to establish clear boundaries and communication channels. While the traditional rule may no longer be a hard line, the underlying principle of protecting portfolio interests remains a priority.

Industry Reactions

Other venture firms have echoed Benchmark’s concerns. Several have begun to draft more nuanced investment agreements that address the unique challenges posed by AI, such as shared data sets and overlapping talent pools. Some are also exploring joint investment structures that allow multiple firms to co‑invest in a single AI startup while maintaining clear governance frameworks.

Looking Ahead

As AI continues to mature, the venture‑capital community will likely need to refine its approach to conflict of interest. Benchmark’s candid acknowledgment of the problem signals a shift toward more transparent and collaborative investment practices. The outcome of this evolution will shape how AI startups secure funding and how investors manage their portfolios in an increasingly interconnected ecosystem.
