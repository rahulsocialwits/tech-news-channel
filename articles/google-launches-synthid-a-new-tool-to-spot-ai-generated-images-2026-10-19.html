Google Launches SynthID: A New Tool to Spot AI‑Generated Images

2026-10-19 · 3 min read
Google’s SynthID lets users upload photos to check if they were created or altered by AI, offering a quick web‑based verification method.

Google has rolled out a new web service called SynthID that allows anyone to upload an image and receive a quick assessment of whether the picture was generated or manipulated by artificial intelligence. The tool is designed to be simple to use, requiring only a single click to submit a photo and then displaying a confidence score that indicates the likelihood of AI involvement.

How SynthID Works

Users can drag and drop an image into the SynthID interface or paste a URL. Once the file is uploaded, the system runs a series of checks that compare the image against a database of known AI‑generated patterns. The result is presented as a percentage, with higher values suggesting a stronger probability that the image was produced by an AI model. The service also provides a brief explanation of the factors that contributed to the score, such as texture inconsistencies or unnatural lighting.

Use Cases and Limitations

While SynthID is marketed as a quick way to verify authenticity, the developers caution that it is not foolproof. The tool can flag images that have been heavily edited or that contain elements from multiple sources, leading to false positives. Conversely, highly sophisticated AI models may still slip past the detection algorithms, resulting in false negatives. The service is intended as a first line of defense rather than a definitive forensic tool.

Privacy and Data Handling

Google states that uploaded images are processed in the cloud and are not stored permanently. The company emphasizes that the service is designed to respect user privacy, with no retention of the original files beyond the brief analysis period. Users can also delete their submissions manually if they wish to remove the data from the system entirely.

Availability and Future Plans

SynthID is currently available to the public on Google’s website, with no subscription or payment required. The company has indicated plans to expand the tool’s capabilities in the coming months, including support for video content and deeper integration with other Google products such as Search and Photos. As AI‑generated media becomes more prevalent, tools like SynthID are expected to play an increasingly important role in media verification and digital literacy efforts.
