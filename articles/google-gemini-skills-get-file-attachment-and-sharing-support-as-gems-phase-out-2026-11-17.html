Google Gemini Skills Get File Attachment and Sharing Support as Gems Phase Out

2026-11-17 · 3 min read
Google expands Gemini Skills with file attachment and sharing features, marking the end of Gems in personal accounts.

Google has announced a significant update to its Gemini AI ecosystem, adding file attachment and sharing capabilities to Gemini Skills. The move comes as the company phases out the Gems feature for personal accounts, which will become read‑only today and disappear next month.

What’s New for Gemini Skills?

Gemini Skills, the AI‑powered add‑ons that let users extend Gemini’s functionality, now support attaching files directly within the chat interface. Users can upload documents, spreadsheets, PDFs, and other file types, allowing the AI to analyze and reference content in real time. In addition, a new sharing option lets users send the file‑enhanced skill to others, enabling collaborative workflows without leaving the Gemini environment.

Why the Change?

Google’s decision to retire Gems in personal accounts follows a broader strategy to streamline its AI offerings. By consolidating features into Gemini Skills, the company aims to provide a more cohesive experience for both individual and business users. The read‑only status of Gems today signals the transition period, after which the feature will be removed entirely.

Impact on Users

For everyday users, the new file attachment feature means Gemini can now handle more complex queries that involve data from external documents. For example, a user could upload a financial report and ask the AI to summarize key metrics or compare figures across multiple sheets. The sharing capability also opens up possibilities for team collaboration, as users can share a skill that includes a specific file with colleagues who can then interact with the same AI context.

Business and Enterprise Implications

Enterprise customers who rely on Gemini for internal knowledge management stand to benefit from the enhanced file handling. The ability to attach and share files directly within the AI chat reduces the need for separate document‑sharing tools, potentially improving productivity and reducing data silos. Moreover, the phased retirement of Gems aligns with Google’s focus on enterprise‑grade AI solutions, which may include tighter security and compliance controls.

Looking Ahead

Google has not yet outlined a full roadmap for Gemini Skills, but the addition of file attachments and sharing is a clear signal that the platform will continue to evolve. Users can expect further integrations with Google Workspace and other productivity tools, as the company seeks to embed AI more deeply into everyday workflows.
