Decoding Netgear Router LEDs: What Each Light Really Means

2026-10-19 · 3 min read
A guide to understanding the LED indicators on Netgear routers and how to troubleshoot common issues.

Netgear routers are a staple in home and small‑business networks, but their LED panels can be confusing for users who aren’t familiar with the symbols. The company’s support pages explain that each light represents a different function, from power status to internet connectivity. Understanding these indicators can help you diagnose problems without needing to dive into the router’s web interface.

Power and Internet Status

The most prominent LEDs on a Netgear router are the power and internet lights. The power LED is usually solid green when the unit is on and turns amber or off if there’s a power issue. The internet LED, often labeled “Internet” or “WAN,” turns solid green when the router has a successful connection to your ISP. If it blinks, the router is attempting to establish a connection; if it’s off, the router can’t reach the internet.

Wi‑Fi and Local Network Indicators

Netgear’s Wi‑Fi LEDs show the status of the wireless networks. A solid green light indicates that the Wi‑Fi radio is active and broadcasting. A blinking light means the router is transmitting data. If the Wi‑Fi LED is off, the wireless radio is disabled or the router is in a low‑power state. The local network LED, often labeled “LAN,” lights up when a device is connected to one of the router’s Ethernet ports. A blinking LAN light indicates data traffic on that port.

Security and VPN Lights

Some Netgear models include a dedicated security LED. When the router is protecting the network with its built‑in firewall or VPN, this light will be solid green. If the security LED is off or blinking, the router may be in a configuration mode or experiencing a security alert. Users should consult the router’s manual for specific meanings, as the symbols can vary between models.

Common Troubleshooting Scenarios

1. Internet LED off, power LED solid green: The router is powered but can’t reach the ISP. Check the cable connections, restart the modem, and verify that the ISP is not experiencing outages.

2. Wi‑Fi LED blinking, no devices connect: The router is broadcasting, but devices can’t join the network. Ensure the SSID and password are correct, and that the router’s firmware is up to date.

3. LAN LED off, no wired devices work: The Ethernet ports may be disabled in the router’s settings, or the cables could be faulty. Try a different cable or port.

When to Contact Support

If the LEDs show unexpected behavior—such as a power LED that flickers or a security LED that stays on continuously—contact Netgear support. The company’s help center provides troubleshooting guides and firmware updates that can resolve many LED-related issues.
