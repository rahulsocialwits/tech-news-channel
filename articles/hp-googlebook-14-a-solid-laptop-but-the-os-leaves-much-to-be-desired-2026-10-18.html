HP Googlebook 14: A Solid Laptop, But the OS Leaves Much to Be Desired

2026-10-18 · 3 min read
HP’s first Googlebook laptop delivers on hardware but falls short on its Googlebook operating system, according to a recent review.

HP has entered the Googlebook arena with the Googlebook 14, a 14‑inch laptop that promises a blend of portability, performance, and a new operating system built on Google’s Android foundation. The device arrives with a 12th‑generation Intel Core i5 processor, 8 GB of RAM, and a 512‑GB SSD, positioning it as a mid‑range contender for students and casual users.

Hardware That Meets Expectations

The Googlebook 14’s chassis is lightweight at just 1.3 kg, and its 14‑inch Full HD display offers a 1920×1080 resolution with a 60‑Hz refresh rate. HP has also equipped the laptop with a back‑lit keyboard, a fingerprint reader, and a full set of ports, including USB‑C, USB‑A, HDMI, and a headphone jack. The review notes that the build quality feels solid, and the battery life reaches up to 10 hours under moderate usage.

Software: A Mixed Bag

While the hardware is solid, the Googlebook operating system—an Android‑based platform with a desktop‑like interface—has been a point of contention. The review highlights that the OS lacks native support for many desktop applications, and the app ecosystem is still in its infancy. Users report that popular productivity tools such as Microsoft Office and Adobe Creative Cloud are either unavailable or require workarounds.

Another issue is the limited ability to install third‑party software outside the Google Play Store. The review points out that this restriction hampers the device’s versatility, especially for power users who rely on specialized tools.

Performance and Usability

Under load, the Googlebook 14 performs comparably to other laptops in its class. The Intel Core i5 processor handles everyday tasks such as web browsing, document editing, and light photo editing without noticeable lag. However, the device struggles with more demanding applications, such as video rendering or gaming, due to its integrated graphics and limited RAM.

Battery life is respectable, but the review notes that heavy multitasking can drain the battery faster than expected. The device’s thermal performance is adequate, with the fan remaining quiet during normal use.

Conclusion: A Good Choice for Some, Not All

HP’s Googlebook 14 offers a compelling package for users who prioritize portability and a clean, touch‑friendly interface. However, the operating system’s limitations and the lack of a robust app ecosystem make it a less attractive option for users who need full desktop functionality. As Google continues to refine the Googlebook OS, the device may become a more viable alternative to traditional Windows laptops in the future.
