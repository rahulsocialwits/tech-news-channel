Google’s Gemini App to Replace Gems with Skills in November

2026-09-27 · 3 min read
Google is shifting from custom Gemini versions called Gems to a new skill‑based system in its Gemini app, set to launch in November.

Google is preparing a major update to its Gemini AI platform that will replace the custom “Gems” it introduced in 2024 with a new system of “skills.” The change is slated for release in November and is part of the company’s effort to make the Gemini experience more modular and user‑friendly.

What Are Gems and Why Replace Them?

Gems were essentially personalized versions of Gemini that users could tailor for specific tasks or industries. While they offered flexibility, the process of creating and managing multiple Gems proved cumbersome for many users. By moving to a skill‑based model, Google aims to streamline the experience, allowing users to add or remove discrete capabilities without the overhead of maintaining separate Gem instances.

How the Skill System Works

In the new setup, each skill functions as a plug‑in that can be activated or deactivated within the Gemini app. Users can browse a catalog of skills, ranging from advanced data analysis tools to creative writing assistants, and integrate them into their workflow with a few taps. This modular approach is expected to reduce the learning curve and make it easier for businesses to adopt Gemini for specific use cases.

Implications for Developers and Businesses

Developers who previously built custom Gems will need to adapt their workflows to the new skill framework. Google has indicated that the transition will be supported with documentation and migration tools, but the exact timeline for developers to shift their offerings remains unclear. For businesses, the skill model promises quicker deployment and lower maintenance costs, as they can now add or remove capabilities on demand.

Industry Reaction

Tech analysts have noted that the move aligns with a broader industry trend toward modular AI services. By simplifying the user experience, Google hopes to attract a wider audience, from individual creators to large enterprises, who may have been deterred by the complexity of managing multiple Gems.

Next Steps for Users

Users of the Gemini app should expect a notification in the coming weeks about the upcoming November release. The update will include a new interface for skill management and guidance on how to transition from existing Gems. Those who have not yet adopted Gemini can explore the new skill catalog to determine which capabilities best fit their needs.
