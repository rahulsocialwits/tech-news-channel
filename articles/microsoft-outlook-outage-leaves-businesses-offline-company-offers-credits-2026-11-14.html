Microsoft Outlook Outage Leaves Businesses Offline, Company Offers Credits

2026-11-14 · 3 min read
Microsoft’s recent outage of Outlook and other business services caused downtime for users, prompting the company to offer partial credits on future bills.

Last week, Microsoft’s Outlook and other business services went offline for many users, causing significant disruption for companies that rely on the platform for email and collaboration. The outage, which began on November 8, was attributed to a software issue that impacted the company’s cloud‑based services.

Impact on Enterprises

Business users reported that they could not access email, calendar, or other Office 365 features during the outage. Microsoft acknowledged the problem and stated that it was monitoring the backlog of email messages that accumulated during the event before considering the issue resolved.

Customer Compensation

In response to the downtime, Microsoft announced that affected customers would receive a partial credit on their next monthly bill. The company’s spokesperson said the credit would be applied automatically, and customers could see the adjustment in their billing statements.

Recovery and Monitoring

Microsoft confirmed that its consumer products services had recovered, but the company is still monitoring enterprise services to ensure stability. The incident has prompted a review of the company’s cloud infrastructure and incident response procedures.
