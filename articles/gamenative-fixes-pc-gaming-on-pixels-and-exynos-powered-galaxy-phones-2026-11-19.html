GameNative Fixes PC Gaming on Pixels and Exynos‑Powered Galaxy Phones

2026-11-19 · 3 min read
GameNative’s latest update brings major PC gaming fixes to Google Pixel and Exynos Galaxy devices, expanding the platform’s reach beyond Snapdragon hardware.

GameNative, the cross‑platform gaming service that lets players run PC titles on Android devices, has rolled out a new update that removes a long‑standing limitation: the need for Snapdragon processors. The patch brings significant performance and compatibility fixes to Google Pixel phones and Exynos‑powered Samsung Galaxy models, broadening the service’s appeal to a wider range of Android hardware.

What the update changes

The update addresses several key issues that had frustrated users on non‑Snapdragon devices. First, it improves the integration of the GameNative engine with Exynos chipsets, reducing input lag and stuttering in fast‑paced games. Second, the patch enhances the graphics pipeline, allowing higher frame rates and better visual fidelity on Pixel and Galaxy screens. Finally, it adds support for a broader set of DirectX and Vulkan features, ensuring that more PC titles run smoothly.

Why it matters for gamers

Until now, GameNative’s flagship feature—playing PC games on a phone—was largely confined to devices with Qualcomm’s Snapdragon processors. By opening the platform to Exynos and Pixel hardware, the company removes a major barrier for users who own Samsung or Google phones. This move also positions GameNative as a more competitive alternative to other mobile gaming services that rely on cloud streaming.

Future outlook

GameNative’s CEO, who has emphasized the company’s commitment to expanding its hardware compatibility, said the update is part of a broader strategy to make PC gaming more accessible on Android. The company plans to continue refining performance on a wider array of devices and to add support for additional gaming peripherals.
