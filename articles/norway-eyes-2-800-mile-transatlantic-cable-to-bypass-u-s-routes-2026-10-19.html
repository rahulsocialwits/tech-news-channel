Norway Eyes 2,800‑mile Transatlantic Cable to Bypass U.S. Routes

2026-10-19 · 3 min read
Bulk Infrastructure Group seeks partner for new undersea fiber link between Canada and Europe, aiming to reduce reliance on U.S. cables.

Norway’s Bulk Infrastructure Group AS (BIG) has announced plans to build a 2,800‑mile undersea fiber‑optic cable that would connect Canada to Europe, bypassing the United States. The project, which would cost roughly $620 million, is part of a broader strategy to diversify transatlantic data routes and reduce dependence on U.S.‑controlled infrastructure.

Why a new route matters

Current transatlantic links largely run through U.S. territory, giving the United States a strategic advantage in controlling data traffic between North America and Europe. By establishing a direct Canada‑to‑Europe connection, BIG aims to provide an alternative path that could improve resilience, reduce latency for certain traffic flows, and offer a more politically neutral route for data exchanges.

Seeking partners and funding

BIG is actively looking for a partner to help fund and build the cable. The company has identified a Canadian majority partner as a potential collaborator, hoping to secure the necessary capital and technical expertise to bring the project to fruition. The partnership would also help navigate regulatory approvals in both Canada and the European Union.

Technical overview

The proposed cable would span the North Atlantic, linking Canadian coastal points to European landing stations. While the exact route is still under study, the design would incorporate the latest fiber‑optic technology to support high‑capacity data transmission and future upgrades. The project is expected to enhance connectivity for businesses, cloud providers, and consumers in both regions.

Implications for the industry

Should the project proceed, it could shift the balance of power in global data routing. Companies that rely on transatlantic bandwidth may gain new options for redundancy and cost optimization. Moreover, the initiative could spur further investment in alternative undersea routes, encouraging a more distributed and secure global internet infrastructure.
