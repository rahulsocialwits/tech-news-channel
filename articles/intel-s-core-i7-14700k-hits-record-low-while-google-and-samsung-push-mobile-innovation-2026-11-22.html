Intel’s Core i7‑14700K Hits Record Low, While Google and Samsung Push Mobile Innovation

2026-11-22 · 3 min read
Intel’s Core i7‑14700K drops to $249.99, and Google and Samsung release new Pixel 11 and Galaxy Z Fold 8 models that reshape the mid‑range and foldable markets.

In a move that could reshape the PC market, Intel has slashed the price of its flagship Raptor Lake refresh, the Core i7‑14700K, to a record low of $249.99. The price cut comes after months of inconsistent pricing and follows the announcement that the processor supports both DDR4 and DDR5 memory on LGA 1700 motherboards. The move is aimed at keeping the brand competitive against AMD’s Ryzen 7000 series while offering a high‑performance option for gamers and content creators on a budget.

Why the Price Drop Matters

The Core i7‑14700K is a 14‑core, 20‑thread CPU that can boost up to 5.0 GHz. It delivers strong single‑thread performance and ample multi‑thread throughput, making it a popular choice for gaming rigs and workstations. By lowering the price to $249.99, Intel is targeting the mid‑range segment where consumers are looking for a balance between performance and cost. The processor’s support for both DDR4 and DDR5 also gives buyers flexibility in choosing motherboards that fit their budget or future‑proofing plans.

Google’s Pixel 11: A Worthwhile Upgrade?

While Intel is making waves in the PC space, Google is pushing the Pixel line forward with the Pixel 11. According to Engadget, the new phone offers a more refined camera system, a faster processor, and a slimmer design compared to the Pixel 9 and 10. However, the article notes that the incremental improvements may not justify the price jump for every user, especially in a market where many mid‑range phones offer similar specs at lower prices. Consumers are advised to weigh the benefits of the new hardware against the cost before upgrading.

Samsung’s Galaxy Z Fold 8: A Practical Foldable

Samsung’s latest foldable, the Galaxy Z Fold 8, is described by 9to5Google as a more user‑friendly iteration of the foldable form factor. The device features a larger, more robust hinge and a slimmer overall design, making it easier to handle. It also introduces new software optimizations that improve multitasking and app continuity across the folded and unfolded states. The Z Fold 8 is positioned as a premium device that offers a unique productivity experience for professionals and power users.

Audio Innovation: Sonos Beam (Gen 2) Hits a Record Low

In the audio arena, Sonos has reduced the price of its compact Dolby Atmos soundbar, the Beam (Gen 2), to $339. Android Authority reports that this price is $10 below the previous best price on Amazon, offering a significant discount for consumers looking to upgrade their home theater setups. The Beam (Gen 2) retains the same high‑quality audio performance as its predecessor while adding new voice assistant integrations and improved wireless connectivity.

What This Means for Consumers

Across the board, these price adjustments and product releases signal a broader trend of manufacturers offering more value for money. Intel’s aggressive pricing strategy could spur a price war in the CPU market, while Google and Samsung continue to push the envelope in mobile innovation. Meanwhile, Sonos’s discount makes premium audio more accessible to a wider audience. Together, these moves highlight the competitive nature of today’s technology landscape, where companies must balance performance, innovation, and affordability to capture market share.
