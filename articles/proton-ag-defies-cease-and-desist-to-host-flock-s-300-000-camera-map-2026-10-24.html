Proton AG Defies Cease‑and‑Desist to Host Flock’s 300,000‑Camera Map

2026-10-24 · 3 min read
Privacy‑focused Proton AG has agreed to host the Flock camera map, exposing over 300,000 cameras worldwide after legal threats from the creator’s former employer.

In a bold move that underscores the tension between privacy advocacy and corporate control, Proton AG has decided to host the Flock camera map on its Swiss servers. The map, which lists more than 300,000 cameras worldwide, was originally published by a security researcher who had been threatened with cease‑and‑desist orders by the company that once employed him.

What the Map Reveals

The Flock map aggregates publicly accessible IP cameras, providing users with a visual representation of camera locations across the globe. By making the data available on Proton’s infrastructure, the company has given privacy advocates a tool to identify who is recording in public and private spaces.

Legal Backdrop

After the researcher released the map, the former employer issued legal notices demanding removal. Proton’s decision to host the map signals a commitment to transparency and user rights, aligning with its broader privacy‑first philosophy.

Implications for Users

While the map offers a powerful way to monitor surveillance, it also raises questions about the legality of publicly sharing camera locations. Users accessing the map should be aware of local laws regarding privacy and data sharing.

Proton’s Position

Proton AG stated that hosting the map is consistent with its mission to protect user privacy and promote open access to information. The company emphasized that it will continue to defend the map against future legal challenges.
