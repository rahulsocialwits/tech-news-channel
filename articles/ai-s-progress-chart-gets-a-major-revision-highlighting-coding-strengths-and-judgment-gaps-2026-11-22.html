AI’s Progress Chart Gets a Major Revision, Highlighting Coding Strengths and Judgment Gaps

2026-11-22 · 3 min read
A new METR report updates the AI time‑horizon graph, showing rapid coding gains but slower progress on judgment tasks.

In a recent release, the AI evaluation nonprofit METR has updated its long‑standing time‑horizon plot, a visual representation of how quickly artificial intelligence models are expected to reach various milestones. The revision, published on November 20, 2026, reflects fresh data from the organization’s latest assessment of a broad range of AI capabilities.

What the Updated Graph Shows

The updated chart maintains the familiar X‑axis of time and the Y‑axis of capability, but it now includes a clearer distinction between tasks that rely on coding and those that require human‑like judgment. According to METR, models are advancing at an exponential rate in coding‑related tasks, such as generating functional code snippets, debugging, and automating routine software development workflows. In contrast, the graph indicates that progress on judgment‑heavy tasks—like nuanced decision making, ethical reasoning, and complex problem solving—has been more incremental.

Implications for Developers and Businesses

For software engineers, the update reinforces the growing utility of AI assistants in coding environments. Tools that can auto‑complete code, suggest optimizations, or even write boilerplate from natural language prompts are expected to become increasingly reliable in the near term. Companies that rely on rapid software iteration may find that integrating such AI capabilities can shave weeks off development cycles.

On the other hand, the slower pace in judgment tasks suggests that AI will still need human oversight for high‑stakes decisions. This includes areas like legal compliance, medical diagnostics, and strategic business planning, where nuanced judgment and contextual understanding remain critical.

Why the Update Matters

METR’s report is significant because it provides a data‑driven lens on where AI is heading, helping stakeholders set realistic expectations. The organization’s time‑horizon plot has long been a reference point for investors, policymakers, and technologists. By refining the graph, METR offers a more nuanced view that can guide resource allocation and research priorities.

Looking Ahead

While the updated chart underscores the rapid gains in coding, it also highlights the need for continued research into AI’s judgment capabilities. As the industry pushes toward more autonomous systems, understanding these limitations will be essential for building safe and effective AI solutions.
