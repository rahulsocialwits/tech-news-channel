Google Home Speakers Found Downloading Hundreds of Gigabytes in Background

2026-10-20 · 3 min read
Some Google Home speakers are silently downloading massive amounts of data, raising privacy and bandwidth concerns.

Google Home speakers have been discovered downloading hundreds of gigabytes of data in the background, according to a report from 9to5Google. The issue appears to affect a subset of owners, with the data being pulled without user consent or clear notification.

What’s Happening?

When users connect a new Google Home speaker to their Wi‑Fi network, the device initiates a series of background downloads. In some cases, the downloads reach several hundred gigabytes, far exceeding the normal firmware or update traffic expected from a smart speaker.

Potential Impact

Large data transfers can strain home networks, consume significant bandwidth, and raise privacy questions about what content is being fetched. The source of the downloads has not been identified, and Google has not yet released a public statement on the matter.

What Users Can Do

Owners experiencing unexpected data usage can:

  • Check the device’s network activity in the Google Home app.
  • Reset the speaker to factory settings and re‑pair it to see if the issue persists.
  • Monitor bandwidth usage on their router to identify the source of the traffic.

Google has not yet confirmed whether the downloads are related to firmware updates, content caching, or a potential bug. Users are advised to keep their devices updated and to report the issue through the Google Home support channels.
