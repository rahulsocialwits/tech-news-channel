New York Times Cooking Adds AI‑Powered Recipe Modifier to Help Home Cooks

2026-11-09 · 3 min read
The New York Times’ Cooking app now offers a beta feature that lets users swap ingredients and adjust recipes using AI, improving accessibility and personalization.

The New York Times has rolled out a beta feature in its Cooking app that lets users tweak recipes on the fly. By selecting a dish, readers can now swap ingredients or adjust quantities, and the app’s AI engine suggests suitable alternatives that keep the flavor profile intact.

How It Works

When a user chooses to modify a recipe, the app analyzes the original ingredient list and the requested changes. It then searches a database of ingredient substitutions and calculates how the adjustments affect taste, texture, and nutrition. The result is a new recipe that the user can save or share.

Why It Matters

Home cooks often face challenges when a recipe calls for an ingredient that’s hard to find or doesn’t fit dietary restrictions. The AI‑powered modifier reduces trial and error, making cooking more inclusive and less wasteful.

Early Feedback

Initial reviews suggest the feature “mostly hits the mark,” with users praising its accuracy and ease of use. The New York Times plans to refine the tool based on user feedback before a full rollout.
