Meta Begins Charging Government and Nonprofit Pages to Post Links on Facebook

2026-10-24 · 3 min read
Meta introduces a new fee for government and nonprofit pages that want to share links on Facebook, a move that could reshape how public entities use the platform.

Meta has announced a new policy that will require government and nonprofit pages to pay a fee to post links on Facebook. The change, which is being rolled out in stages, is part of the company’s broader effort to monetize its platform while maintaining a level playing field for all users.

What the Policy Means

Under the new rules, any public‑sector page that wants to share a link will need to purchase a link‑posting license. The fee will vary depending on the size of the page’s audience and the frequency of link sharing. Meta has stated that the policy is designed to be “transparent and predictable,” and that it will not affect the ability of these pages to post other types of content.

Why It Matters

Government and nonprofit organizations have long relied on Facebook to reach constituents and supporters. The introduction of a fee could change how these entities allocate their digital budgets and may prompt a shift toward other social media platforms that remain free for public‑sector use.

Meta’s Rationale

In a statement, Meta said the new policy is a “test” that is “explicitly not supposed to impact these entities.” The company emphasized that it is still evaluating the impact of the fee and will adjust the program as needed. Meta also highlighted that the move is part of its broader strategy to diversify revenue streams beyond advertising.

Reactions from the Public Sector

Early responses from government officials and nonprofit leaders have been mixed. Some see the fee as a reasonable cost for the reach and engagement that Facebook offers, while others worry about the financial burden on smaller organizations. The policy is expected to spark debate about the role of social media in public communication and the sustainability of free access for non‑commercial users.
