Free Open‑Source Utility Lets Mac Users Reclaim Gigabytes of Hidden Space

2026-11-08 · 3 min read
An open‑source app cleans hidden caches, app junk and offline Apple Intelligence models, freeing up space on macOS without a paid subscription.

Mac users who have noticed their storage slowly filling up may find relief in a newly highlighted open‑source utility. The tool, discussed in a recent Wired story, safely removes hidden caches, leftover app junk and even large offline Apple Intelligence models that can accumulate over time.

What the App Does

The utility scans the system for files that are no longer needed but remain on the disk. It targets three main categories:

  • Hidden caches that apps leave behind after updates.
  • Residual files from uninstalled applications.
  • Offline models used by Apple’s on‑device intelligence services.

By deleting these files, the app can free up several gigabytes of space, which is especially valuable for users with limited SSD capacity.

Why It Matters

Apple’s recent updates have increased the size of on‑device intelligence models, and many users are unaware that these files can grow large. The open‑source nature of the tool means it can be inspected and modified by the community, ensuring transparency and security.

Getting Started

Users can download the utility from its GitHub repository, where installation instructions are provided. The app runs from the command line and offers a simple interface for selecting which categories to clean.

Community Reception

Early adopters report significant space savings without compromising system stability. The tool has been praised for its straightforward approach and the fact that it eliminates the need for paid third‑party cleaners.
