New York Orders T‑Mobile to Drop Data Caps on Low‑Income Home Internet Plans

2026-11-12 · 3 min read
The city mandates T‑Mobile to stop throttling speeds for Affordable Broadband Act customers, ensuring fair access for low‑income households.

New York City has taken a decisive step to protect low‑income residents from internet throttling. The city’s Department of Consumer Affairs has ordered T‑Mobile to cease imposing data caps on home‑internet plans offered under the Affordable Broadband Act. The move follows a review that found the carrier was throttling speeds for these plans, effectively limiting the bandwidth available to households that rely on the program for affordable connectivity.

What the Order Means

The order requires T‑Mobile to remove any speed restrictions on the affected plans and to provide clear, transparent information about the service levels customers can expect. The carrier must also report compliance to the city within 30 days. If T‑Mobile fails to comply, it could face fines and further regulatory action.

Impact on Low‑Income Households

Affordable Broadband Act plans are designed to give low‑income families access to high‑speed internet at a reduced cost. By eliminating data caps, the city aims to ensure that these households can use the internet for work, education, and essential services without unexpected slowdowns.

Industry Reactions

Telecom analysts say the ruling could prompt other states to review their own broadband policies. T‑Mobile has not yet issued a public statement, but industry observers expect the company to comply to avoid reputational damage.
