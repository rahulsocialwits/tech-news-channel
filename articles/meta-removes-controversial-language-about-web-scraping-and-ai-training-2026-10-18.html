Meta Removes Controversial Language About Web Scraping and AI Training

2026-10-18 · 3 min read
Meta has updated its terms of service to clarify that its web crawling practices remain unchanged, addressing concerns over data scraping and AI model training.

Meta has taken a step to quell growing unease over how it harvests data from the web for its artificial‑intelligence projects. In a statement released on Saturday, the company announced that it has removed a clause from its terms of service that many critics said implied a shift in its crawling policy. The change comes amid a broader debate about the ethics of using publicly available content to train large language models.

What the Update Actually Says

The removed language, which had been in place since 2024, suggested that Meta might expand its web‑scraping activities to gather more data for AI training. Meta’s spokesperson clarified that the removal does not reflect any change in the company’s approach to crawling the web. The company reiterated that it continues to follow the same guidelines it has used for years, which include respecting robots.txt directives and avoiding the collection of personal data without consent.

Why the Clause Caused a Stir

When the clause first appeared, privacy advocates and AI ethicists argued that it could give Meta a legal cover to scrape larger swaths of the internet. They pointed out that the clause’s wording was vague enough to be interpreted as a potential expansion of data collection. The clause also raised questions about how the data would be used in training Meta’s AI models, which are increasingly integrated into products like Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp.

Industry Reactions

TechCrunch reported that the update was a response to mounting pressure from regulators and civil‑society groups. While the company has not yet issued a formal policy statement on AI training data, the removal of the contentious wording is seen as a gesture toward transparency. Some analysts note that Meta’s move could help it avoid potential legal challenges in jurisdictions that are tightening rules around data usage for AI.

What This Means for Users

For everyday users, the change is unlikely to affect how they interact with Meta’s services. The company’s data‑collection practices remain governed by its existing privacy policy, which outlines the types of data collected and how it is used. Users who are concerned about their data can still review Meta’s privacy settings and opt out of certain data collection practices.

Looking Ahead

Meta’s decision to remove the clause comes at a time when the tech industry is under scrutiny for how it handles data for AI. The company’s next steps will likely involve clarifying its data‑usage policies in more detail, especially as it continues to develop new AI features across its platforms. For now, the removal of the controversial language is a small but notable shift in Meta’s approach to transparency and data ethics.
