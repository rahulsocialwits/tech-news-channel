WhatsApp Web Sessions on Public Computers Pose New Security Risk, Warns Indian Government

2026-11-23 · 3 min read
The Indian government warns users that leaving WhatsApp Web sessions open on public computers can expose accounts to hijacking, urging manual log‑outs after each use.

In a recent advisory, the National Cybersecurity and Technology Authority (NCTAU) and the Indian Institute for Cybersecurity (I4C) highlighted a growing threat to WhatsApp users in India. The warning focuses on the risk of account hijacking when a WhatsApp Web session is left logged in on a public or shared computer.

What the Advisory Says

According to the government notice, simply closing a browser window or tab does not terminate an authenticated WhatsApp Web session. The session remains active until the user explicitly logs out. This oversight can allow anyone with physical access to the computer to hijack the account, read messages, and potentially impersonate the user.

Why It Matters

WhatsApp Web is widely used for business and personal communication. The platform’s popularity means that many users rely on public terminals—such as those in libraries, cafés, or coworking spaces—to access their accounts. If a session remains active, a malicious actor can exploit it to gain unauthorized access to sensitive conversations and personal data.

Recommended Precautions

  • Always log out manually: After finishing a session, click the profile icon and select “Log out” to terminate the connection.
  • Use private browsing modes: While not a substitute for logging out, private or incognito windows reduce the chance of session data being stored.
  • Enable two‑factor authentication: Adding an extra verification step can help prevent unauthorized access even if a session is hijacked.
  • Educate users: Organizations and educational institutions should inform students and staff about the risks of leaving sessions open.

Broader Context

The advisory comes amid increasing scrutiny of digital privacy and security in India. As more services migrate to web‑based interfaces, the importance of session management and user awareness grows. The NCTAU’s guidance aligns with global best practices that emphasize explicit log‑out procedures and user education to mitigate session‑based attacks.

Next Steps for Users

WhatsApp has not issued a direct response to the advisory, but the platform’s design already includes a “Log out” option. Users should make it a habit to use this feature, especially when accessing the service from shared devices. By following the government’s recommendations, Indian users can protect themselves against potential account hijacking and maintain the integrity of their personal communications.
