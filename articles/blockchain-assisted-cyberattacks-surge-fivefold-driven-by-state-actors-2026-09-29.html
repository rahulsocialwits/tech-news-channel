Blockchain‑Assisted Cyberattacks Surge Fivefold, Driven by State Actors

2026-09-29 · 3 min read
A new report shows a 440% rise in blockchain‑based cyberattacks, with Iranian, North Korean and Russia‑linked groups using smart contracts to hide malware and command‑and‑control infrastructure.

A recent study released by Tom's Hardware reveals a dramatic increase in blockchain‑assisted cyberattacks. The report documents a 440% surge in dead‑drop attacks that leverage public blockchains to conceal malware payloads and command‑and‑control (C2) infrastructure.

State‑Linked Actors at the Forefront

According to the analysis, Iranian and North Korean state actors are among the most active in deploying these techniques. Russian‑linked groups also feature prominently, using smart contracts and phantom wallets to obfuscate their operations.

How the Attacks Work

Attackers embed malicious code within blockchain transactions or smart contracts, allowing them to remain hidden from traditional security tools. The public nature of the blockchain makes it difficult for defenders to trace the origin of the malware or the command signals sent to compromised systems.

Implications for Security Teams

Security professionals are urged to monitor blockchain activity more closely and to integrate blockchain analytics into their threat‑intelligence workflows. The rise in these attacks underscores the need for advanced detection capabilities that can identify malicious patterns within decentralized networks.
