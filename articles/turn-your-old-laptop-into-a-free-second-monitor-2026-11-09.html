Turn Your Old Laptop Into a Free Second Monitor

2026-11-09 · 3 min read
Learn how to wirelessly or wiredly connect a spare laptop to your main machine for a dual‑display setup without extra hardware.

Many people have an unused laptop lying around, but few realize it can double as a second screen for their primary machine. By following a few simple steps, you can turn that spare device into a free dual‑monitor setup, whether you prefer a wired connection or a wireless one.

Why Use a Laptop as a Second Monitor?

Expanding your workspace can boost productivity, especially for tasks that benefit from side‑by‑side windows, such as coding, design, or multitasking with chat and email. A second screen also helps keep reference material visible while you work on the main display.

Wired Connection Setup

Connecting via a USB‑C or HDMI cable is the most reliable method. Plug one end into the primary laptop’s output port and the other into the spare laptop’s input. Once both devices are powered on, the spare laptop will detect the external signal and can be set to mirror or extend the display in its display settings.

Wireless Connection Options

For a cable‑free experience, you can use software solutions that transmit the display over Wi‑Fi. Popular tools include Spacedesk and Duet Display, which allow the spare laptop to act as a secondary screen with minimal lag. Ensure both laptops are on the same network and install the corresponding client and server applications.

Performance Tips

  • Use a high‑speed Wi‑Fi connection (5 GHz) to reduce latency.
  • Close unnecessary applications on the spare laptop to free up resources.
  • Adjust the resolution and scaling settings on the secondary display to match your workflow.

When to Consider a Dedicated Monitor

While a laptop can serve as a quick and cost‑effective second screen, dedicated monitors offer better ergonomics, higher refresh rates, and more precise color accuracy. If you plan to use a dual‑display setup regularly, investing in a monitor may be worthwhile.
