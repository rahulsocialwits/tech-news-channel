Oakland Startup Aims to End China’s Graphite Monopoly

2026-11-18 · 3 min read
Molten Industries plans a new Oakland factory that could challenge China’s near‑total control of battery graphite supply.

Chinese firms have long dominated the global graphite market, supplying the bulk of the raw material used in lithium‑ion batteries. Molten Industries, a U.S. startup, is now positioning itself to disrupt that monopoly with a new production facility in Oakland, California.

Why Graphite Matters

Graphite is a key component of anode material in rechargeable batteries. Its supply chain has been a strategic concern for governments and manufacturers alike, as any disruption can ripple through the entire electric‑vehicle and energy‑storage industries.

Molten’s Oakland Plan

According to a TechCrunch report, Molten Industries intends to build a factory that will produce high‑purity graphite at scale. The company claims its process can deliver material that meets the stringent specifications required by top battery makers, potentially offering a domestic alternative to imports.

Potential Impact on the Market

If successful, the Oakland plant could reduce U.S. dependence on Chinese graphite, providing a more resilient supply chain for battery manufacturers. It may also spur further investment in domestic graphite production, encouraging other startups to enter the space.

Challenges Ahead

Building a competitive graphite plant involves significant capital, technical expertise, and regulatory approvals. Molten Industries will need to navigate these hurdles while maintaining cost competitiveness against established Chinese producers.

Industry Reaction

Industry analysts see the move as a strategic push toward supply‑chain diversification. While the startup’s success remains to be seen, its ambition signals a broader shift toward reducing reliance on single‑country dominance in critical battery materials.
