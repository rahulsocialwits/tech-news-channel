Sony Eyes Credit‑Card‑Enabled PlayStation Controllers for Digital‑Only Future

2026-11-15 · 3 min read
Sony’s new patent could let PlayStation controllers read credit cards, streamlining in‑game purchases as the company shifts toward a digital‑only model.

Sony has filed a patent that would allow its next‑generation PlayStation controllers to read credit cards, a move that could make in‑game purchases faster and more convenient for players. The filing, revealed by Engadget, comes as Sony and PlayStation head into a digital‑only era, where physical media is becoming less common.

How the Feature Would Work

The patent describes a small, contact‑less reader built into the controller’s body. When a player taps their card against the controller, the device would transmit payment information to the console, bypassing the need to open a separate app or navigate to a web browser. The system would also support digital wallets, allowing users to link stored payment methods directly to the controller.

Implications for the Gaming Ecosystem

By embedding payment hardware into the controller, Sony could streamline the checkout process for downloadable content, micro‑transactions, and subscription services. The feature would also reduce friction for players who prefer to keep their payment information on the console rather than on a separate device.

Industry Context

Other companies have experimented with similar integrations. For example, Nintendo’s Switch Lite includes a built‑in NFC reader for Amiibo, and some mobile devices feature contact‑less payment chips. Sony’s proposal would bring a comparable convenience to the PlayStation ecosystem.

Next Steps

While the patent is a promising sign of Sony’s intent to simplify digital purchases, the company has not announced a release date or confirmed that the feature will appear on upcoming hardware. Developers will need to adapt their storefronts to support controller‑based payments if the technology moves forward.
