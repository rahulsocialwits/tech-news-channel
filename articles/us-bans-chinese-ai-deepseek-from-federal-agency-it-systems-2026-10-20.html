US Bans Chinese AI DeepSeek from Federal Agency IT Systems

2026-10-20 · 3 min read
The Department of Homeland Security has prohibited the use of the Chinese AI platform DeepSeek on all federal agency websites to mitigate potential security risks.

The U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS) issued a memo in July that now prohibits the use of the Chinese artificial‑intelligence platform DeepSeek on all federal agency websites. The directive requires agencies to monitor traffic in and out of their sites to address potential risks associated with the platform, according to a July memo made public Monday.

Why the ban matters

DeepSeek, a large‑language model developed by a Chinese company, has attracted attention for its advanced natural‑language processing capabilities. DHS officials are concerned that the model could be used to gather sensitive information or facilitate cyber attacks against government infrastructure. By restricting its deployment, the agency aims to reduce the attack surface exposed to foreign actors.

Implications for federal agencies

All federal agencies must now review their current use of DeepSeek and other similar AI services. The memo calls for a comprehensive audit of AI tools integrated into public‑facing websites and internal systems. Agencies that continue to rely on DeepSeek will need to implement additional safeguards or transition to alternative platforms that meet U.S. security standards.

Broader context

The ban reflects a growing trend of U.S. government agencies tightening controls on foreign‑origin AI technologies. Similar actions have been taken against other Chinese AI products, such as the DeepSeek platform, to protect national security and safeguard sensitive data.
