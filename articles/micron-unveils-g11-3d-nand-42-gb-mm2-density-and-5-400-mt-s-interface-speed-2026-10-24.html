Micron Unveils G11 3D NAND: 42 Gb/mm² Density and 5,400 MT/s Interface Speed

2026-10-24 · 3 min read
Micron’s new G11 3D NAND promises double the bit density of current chips and ultra‑fast data rates, paving the way for high‑capacity SSDs.

Micron Technology has announced its next‑generation G11 3D NAND memory, a leap forward in flash storage that delivers a projected 42 gigabits per cubic millimeter density and an interface speed of 5,400 megatransfers per second. The new architecture doubles the bit density of existing 3D NAND products while maintaining the same 400‑layer stack, according to the company’s technical brief.

Key Technical Highlights

  • Bit density: 42 Gb/mm², a 100% increase over the current 21 Gb/mm² standard.
  • Layer count: 400 layers, the same as the current generation, enabling a smoother transition for manufacturers.
  • Interface speed: 5,400 MT/s, matching the performance of the fastest PCIe Gen5 SSDs.
  • Capacity potential: The higher density allows for SSDs with capacities exceeding 20 TB in a single drive form factor.

Implications for the Storage Market

With the G11 3D NAND, Micron aims to address the growing demand for high‑capacity, high‑performance storage in data centers, enterprise servers, and next‑generation consumer devices. The increased density reduces the number of dies needed per drive, potentially lowering manufacturing costs and power consumption. The faster interface speed also aligns with the latest PCIe Gen5 and upcoming Gen6 standards, ensuring compatibility with future platforms.

Industry Response

Industry analysts note that the G11’s specifications place it ahead of competitors such as Samsung’s 3D NAND, which currently offers 30 Gb/mm² density and 4,800 MT/s speeds. Micron’s announcement is expected to spur a wave of new SSD designs that leverage the higher density and speed, particularly in the enterprise and cloud sectors where data throughput and capacity are critical.

Next Steps for Manufacturers

Manufacturers will need to adapt their production lines to accommodate the G11’s 400‑layer stack and new interface protocols. Micron has indicated that the new NAND will be available for production in the second half of 2027, giving OEMs ample time to integrate the technology into upcoming product roadmaps.

Conclusion

Micron’s G11 3D NAND represents a significant milestone in flash memory technology, offering double the density and faster speeds than current offerings. As data centers and high‑performance computing environments continue to push the limits of storage, the G11 is poised to become a cornerstone component in next‑generation SSDs.
