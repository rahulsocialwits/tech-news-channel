Apple HomeView: A Smart Display That’s Still a Step Behind

2026-11-26 · 3 min read
Apple’s $299 HomeView smart display offers a sleek design and useful features, but reviewers say it falls short of competing with established home‑assistant devices.

Apple’s newest entry into the smart‑display market, the HomeView, arrives at a price point of $299 and promises a clean, minimalist experience that fits into any home décor. The device is designed to sit on a kitchen counter or living‑room shelf, offering a 7‑inch touchscreen, a built‑in camera, and a speaker system that supports Apple’s HomeKit ecosystem.

Design and Build

The HomeView’s chassis is a single block of matte black aluminum, giving it a premium feel that matches the aesthetic of other Apple hardware. The screen is a 7‑inch IPS panel with a 1920×1080 resolution, and the device includes a 12‑megapixel camera that can be used for FaceTime calls or as a home‑security camera when paired with HomeKit accessories.

Software and Features

Running on Apple’s custom silicon, the HomeView supports the Home app, Siri, and a range of HomeKit‑compatible smart‑home devices. Users can control lights, thermostats, and locks, view camera feeds, and receive notifications directly on the display. The device also includes a built‑in speaker that delivers clear audio for music and voice commands.

What’s Missing

While the HomeView offers a solid foundation for a smart‑display, reviewers note several areas where it lags behind competitors. The screen size is smaller than many rivals, limiting the amount of information that can be displayed at once. The device also lacks a dedicated voice assistant beyond Siri, meaning it cannot compete with the broader ecosystem of third‑party apps that are available on other platforms. Additionally, the HomeView’s camera is not designed for high‑resolution video streaming, which can be a drawback for users who want to use it as a video‑chat hub.

Verdict

Apple’s HomeView is a well‑built, stylish addition to the smart‑display lineup, but it still has a long way to go before it can match the functionality and flexibility of established players in the market. For Apple fans who value design and integration with HomeKit, it may be worth the $299 price tag, but for those looking for a more feature‑rich experience, other options may be a better fit.
