Veritas Unveils Anti‑Counterfeit Chip for Luxury Goods

2026-10-16 · 3 min read
A new chip from startup Veritas promises to authenticate jewelry, trophies and fragrance bottles, tackling the growing problem of high‑end counterfeit goods.

In a move that could reshape the luxury market, startup Veritas has announced a new anti‑counterfeit chip designed to verify the authenticity of high‑value items such as jewelry, trophies and fragrance bottles. The chip, which can be embedded in a product’s packaging or the item itself, uses a combination of unique identifiers and tamper‑evident features to provide a verifiable proof of origin.

Why the chip matters

Counterfeit luxury goods have long plagued fashion houses and consumers alike. As counterfeiters become more sophisticated, producing “superfakes” that closely mimic genuine products, brands struggle to protect their reputation and revenue. Veritas’ chip offers a technical solution that can be integrated into a wide range of luxury items, giving consumers a simple way to confirm authenticity with a smartphone scan.

How it works

The chip contains a unique serial number that is encrypted and linked to the brand’s database. When a consumer scans the chip with a dedicated app, the app retrieves the serial number and cross‑checks it against the brand’s records. If the number is valid and the chip has not been tampered with, the app displays a green checkmark. If the chip is missing or the data does not match, the app flags the item as potentially counterfeit.

Industry reaction

Industry insiders see the chip as a game‑changer. “Embedding a tamper‑evident chip into luxury goods could become the new standard for authenticity verification,” said a spokesperson for a major jewelry brand. “It gives consumers confidence and protects our intellectual property.”

Challenges ahead

While the technology is promising, widespread adoption will require collaboration between brands, manufacturers and regulators. Costs of embedding chips into every item, ensuring secure data storage, and maintaining consumer privacy are all factors that will need to be addressed. Additionally, counterfeiters may attempt to replicate or bypass the chip, so continuous updates and security patches will be essential.

Looking forward

Veritas plans to roll out the chip in partnership with several luxury brands over the next year. If successful, the technology could extend beyond jewelry and fragrances to include high‑end watches, handbags and even collectibles. By providing a reliable, easy‑to‑use authentication method, Veritas aims to restore trust in the luxury market and protect both consumers and brands from the growing threat of counterfeit goods.
