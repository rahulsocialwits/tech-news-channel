SpaceX Urges Improved Orbital Coordination After Near-Misses with Starlink

2026-10-09 · 3 min read
SpaceX calls for better coordination in orbit following close encounters with its Starlink satellites, highlighting the need for stricter collision avoidance protocols.

SpaceX has publicly called for tighter coordination among space operators after a series of near‑misses involving its Starlink satellite constellation. The company noted that several conjunctions were measured in the range of tens to hundreds of meters, a distance it described as “way too close to comfort.”

What Happened?

During a routine check of its satellite fleet, SpaceX identified multiple close approaches between Starlink satellites and other objects in low Earth orbit. The company’s analysis showed that the separations were far smaller than the safety margins typically maintained by space agencies.

Why It Matters

As the number of active satellites grows, the risk of collisions—and the potential for cascading debris—intensifies. SpaceX’s call for improved coordination underscores the urgency of establishing more robust collision‑avoidance protocols to protect both commercial and governmental space assets.

Industry Response

Other satellite operators and regulatory bodies have begun to review their own tracking and communication procedures. The International Telecommunication Union (ITU) and the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) are expected to weigh in on new guidelines that could standardize how operators share positional data.

Looking Ahead

SpaceX’s statement comes at a time when the commercial space sector is expanding rapidly. With plans for thousands more satellites, the industry faces a growing need for coordinated governance to ensure the long‑term sustainability of space operations.
