Sterilized Soil Holds Life‑Like Chemistry for Six Years, Hinting at Earth’s Origins

2026-11-22 · 3 min read
Scientists discovered that sterilized soil can retain biologically important chemical reactions for six years, offering clues about how life may have begun on Earth.

In a surprising twist, a team of researchers has shown that sterilized soil can preserve complex chemical reactions for an astonishing six years. The study, published in a recent WIRED article, followed a sample of soil that had been subjected to high‑temperature sterilization to eliminate all microbial life. Over the course of six years, the soil continued to exhibit a range of biologically relevant reactions, including the formation of organic molecules that are typically associated with living organisms.

What the Findings Mean for Origin‑of‑Life Research

The persistence of these reactions suggests that the building blocks of life can remain active in a seemingly lifeless environment. This challenges the long‑standing assumption that sterilization completely erases all potential for biochemical activity. By demonstrating that key chemical pathways can survive outside of living cells, the research provides a new lens through which scientists can examine how life might have emerged on early Earth.

Key Chemical Processes Observed

During the six‑year observation period, the soil sample showed:

  • Continuous production of amino‑acid precursors.
  • Formation of simple sugars through non‑enzymatic pathways.
  • Stable concentrations of phosphate compounds, essential for energy transfer.

These processes mirror those that are believed to have taken place in prebiotic environments, offering a tangible model for studying early biochemical evolution.

Implications for Future Studies

Researchers plan to extend the experiment to different soil types and sterilization methods to determine the limits of chemical persistence. The findings also open avenues for exploring how extraterrestrial soils, such as those on Mars or the Moon, might retain prebiotic chemistry over geological timescales.

While the study does not prove that life originated in the soil, it provides compelling evidence that the raw materials and reactions necessary for life can endure far longer than previously thought.
