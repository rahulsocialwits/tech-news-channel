3D Printers Turn Expensive Hobbies Into Cost‑Saving Machines

2026-11-01 · 3 min read
Personal 3D printers can slash costs for hobbyists who spend heavily on supplies, according to recent reviews and pricing deals.

For hobbyists who spend a fortune on materials, a personal 3D printer can be a surprisingly economical investment. Engadget’s recent feature highlighted several high‑end hobbies—such as model building, custom jewelry, and cosplay—where the upfront cost of a printer is offset by the savings on recurring supplies.

When the printer pays for itself

The article notes that while a 3D printer is a significant initial outlay, the cost of filament and other consumables can be far lower than buying pre‑made parts or accessories. For example, a hobbyist who regularly orders custom figurines can print them in a fraction of the price, especially when using high‑quality PLA or PETG filaments that are available in bulk.

Top picks for budget‑savvy makers

Tom’s Hardware recently reviewed the Bambu Lab P1S, an award‑winning printer that offers high‑speed printing and an integrated AMS color system. The review praised its ease of use for beginners and its ability to produce detailed prints quickly, making it a strong contender for hobbyists who want to keep costs down without sacrificing quality.

In addition, the P1S bundle is currently available with a $70 discount, bringing the price closer to the $1,200 range that many hobbyists find acceptable for a reliable machine. The discount is part of a broader trend of manufacturers offering promotional pricing to attract new users.

Why 3D printing saves money

One key advantage is the ability to print replacement parts on demand. Instead of purchasing a new component every time a part fails, users can simply print a replacement, often for a fraction of the retail price. This is especially valuable for intricate or custom pieces that are otherwise expensive to source.

Additionally, 3D printing allows hobbyists to experiment with designs without committing to large orders. Small batch production means users can test prototypes and iterate quickly, reducing waste and the cost of trial and error.

Getting started

For those new to the space, the Bambu Lab P1S’s AMS system simplifies color printing, eliminating the need for manual filament changes. The printer’s software also includes a library of pre‑tested models, which can help beginners avoid costly mistakes.

While the initial investment is higher than buying a single hobby item, the long‑term savings and creative flexibility make 3D printers an attractive option for anyone looking to cut costs while expanding their creative toolkit.
