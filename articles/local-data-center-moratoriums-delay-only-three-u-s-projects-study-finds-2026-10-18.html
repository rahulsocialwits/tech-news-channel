Local Data Center Moratoriums Delay Only Three U.S. Projects, Study Finds

2026-10-18 · 3 min read
A new analysis shows that over 300 local data center bans have stalled just three U.S. projects, highlighting the limited impact of community opposition on the industry.

A recent study by SemiAnalysis has mapped more than 300 local data center moratoriums across the United States and found that only three major projects have been delayed as a result. The research, published on October 18, 2026, indicates that while community opposition to new data centers is vocal, its practical effect on the industry’s expansion plans is surprisingly small.

Key Findings

The analysis compared the number of local moratoriums against the pipeline of data center projects slated to come online by 2027. Despite the high number of bans, the study identified only three projects that experienced measurable delays. These projects, which collectively represent a capacity of just 2–3 gigawatts, were held up by local restrictions that required additional environmental reviews and community engagement.

Industry Response

Data center operators have long cited local opposition as a major hurdle, but the findings suggest that most projects navigate these challenges through site selection, community outreach, and regulatory compliance. The study’s authors argue that the industry’s resilience is due in part to the high demand for cloud infrastructure and the availability of alternative sites.

Implications for the Cloud Market

With the U.S. expected to add 38 gigawatts of data center capacity online in 2027, the limited impact of local bans could ease concerns about supply constraints. However, the study also highlights the importance of transparent engagement with communities to mitigate future opposition and ensure sustainable growth.
