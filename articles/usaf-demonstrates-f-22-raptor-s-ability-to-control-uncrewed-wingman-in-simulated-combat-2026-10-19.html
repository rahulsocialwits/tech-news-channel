USAF Demonstrates F‑22 Raptor’s Ability to Control Uncrewed Wingman in Simulated Combat

2026-10-19 · 3 min read
In a recent test, an F‑22 pilot successfully commanded a YFQ‑44A Fury drone, marking a step toward integrated manned‑unmanned fighter squadrons.

The U.S. Air Force has shown that its fifth‑generation F‑22 Raptor can lead a squadron that includes an unmanned fighter drone. In a simulated combat exercise, a pilot guided a YFQ‑44A Fury drone—developed by Anduril Industries—through a series of air‑to‑air engagements.

Key Highlights of the Test

  • The F‑22 pilot used the aircraft’s existing avionics to issue commands to the drone, demonstrating seamless integration between manned and unmanned platforms.
  • During the exercise, the drone performed complex maneuvers and engaged simulated targets while maintaining coordination with the Raptor.
  • Command and control data were transmitted via the aircraft’s secure network, showing that current systems can support real‑time drone operations.

Implications for Future Fighter Squadrons

Integrating drones into fighter squadrons could increase mission flexibility and reduce pilot risk. The test indicates that the Air Force’s ongoing program to incorporate uncrewed aircraft into combat units is progressing as planned.

Next Steps

Following this demonstration, the Air Force will likely conduct live‑air tests and evaluate the operational benefits of mixed‑manned teams. The results could shape procurement and training strategies for the next decade.
