Pentagon Seeks $30 Million to Fund AI‑Powered Polygraph Next

2026-09-28 · 3 min read
The U.S. Department of Defense is requesting $30.3 million to develop an AI‑enhanced lie detector that uses standoff sensing and machine‑learning scoring algorithms.

The Pentagon has formally requested $30.3 million in its next budget cycle to advance a new line of lie‑detection technology known as Polygraph+ or Polygraph Next. The program will focus on two key innovations: artificial‑intelligence scoring algorithms and a standoff sensing technique that allows the device to assess a subject from a distance.

What the funding will cover

According to the Department of Defense budget request, the $30 million will be spread over five years. The money will go toward research and development of machine‑learning models that can interpret physiological signals more accurately than traditional polygraphs. It will also support the creation of a standoff sensing system that can capture data without the subject being physically present in the same room.

Why it matters

Traditional polygraphs have long been criticized for their limited accuracy and the need for a trained examiner to operate them. By integrating AI, the Pentagon hopes to reduce human error and increase the reliability of the readings. The standoff sensing component could also expand the device’s use in field operations, where rapid, non‑intrusive assessments are critical.

Next steps

If the request is approved, the Pentagon will begin a phased development plan that includes prototype testing and field trials. The program’s success could set a new standard for security screening in both military and civilian contexts.
