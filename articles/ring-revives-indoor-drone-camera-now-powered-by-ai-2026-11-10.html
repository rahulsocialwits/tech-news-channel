Ring Revives Indoor Drone Camera, Now Powered by AI

2026-11-10 · 3 min read
Ring’s new indoor drone camera returns with AI‑driven features, offering homeowners a $250 solution to monitor threats and businesses a Rover Cam with Amazon Astro hardware.

Ring, the home‑security company best known for its doorbell cameras, has re‑introduced its indoor drone camera to the market, this time with a suite of artificial‑intelligence features that promise smarter surveillance and easier operation. The device, priced at $250, can hover around a home, automatically following people and objects while streaming live video to a smartphone app. For commercial users, Ring offers a Rover Cam that incorporates the same core hardware used in Amazon’s former Astro robot, giving businesses a ready‑made solution for indoor monitoring.

AI‑Powered Vision and Navigation

The new drone cam uses computer‑vision algorithms to detect and track people, pets, and even small objects. When a person enters the field of view, the camera automatically locks onto them and follows their movements, maintaining a steady shot without manual control. The AI also powers a “focus mode” that keeps the subject in sharp focus even as the drone moves, reducing the need for users to adjust settings on the fly.

Smart Alerts and Integration

Ring’s software stack now includes AI‑driven alerting. The camera can distinguish between a human intruder and a harmless animal, sending notifications only when a potential threat is detected. Alerts are enriched with contextual data—such as the time of day and the subject’s size—so homeowners can make informed decisions about whether to intervene. The device also integrates with Ring’s existing ecosystem, allowing users to view footage from the drone alongside doorbell and motion‑sensor cameras.

Business‑Grade Rover Cam

For commercial customers, the Rover Cam builds on the same drone platform but adds features tailored to business use. It can be programmed to patrol specific routes, record at set intervals, and store footage in the cloud for later review. The Rover Cam’s hardware is derived from Amazon Astro, giving it a robust chassis and reliable battery life. Businesses can use the device for inventory monitoring, employee safety, or visitor logging.

Competitive Landscape

Ring’s return to the drone market follows a wave of AI‑enhanced security devices from competitors such as DJI and GoPro. While DJI’s Mini series offers high‑resolution video, it lacks the integrated AI tracking that Ring now provides. GoPro’s HERO line focuses on action footage rather than continuous surveillance, leaving a niche for a dedicated indoor drone that can autonomously patrol a space.

Future Outlook

Ring’s move signals a broader trend of embedding AI into consumer hardware to simplify user experience and add value. By automating tracking and alerting, the company hopes to differentiate its product in a crowded market. As AI continues to mature, we can expect further enhancements—such as predictive behavior analysis and deeper integration with smart‑home ecosystems—to make indoor drones an even more compelling option for both homeowners and businesses.
