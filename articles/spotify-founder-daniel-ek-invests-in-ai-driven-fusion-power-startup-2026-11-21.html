Spotify Founder Daniel Ek Invests in AI‑Driven Fusion Power Startup

2026-11-21 · 3 min read
Spotify co‑founder Daniel Ek backs Europa Fusion, a startup founded by an AI researcher aiming to commercialize fusion energy.

Spotify co‑founder Daniel Ek has announced a new investment in Europa Fusion, a startup that seeks to bring fusion power to the commercial market. The company was founded by an AI researcher who has spent the last decade developing machine‑learning models to accelerate plasma physics research.

What Europa Fusion is Building

Europa Fusion’s core technology centers on a compact fusion reactor design that uses advanced AI algorithms to optimize magnetic confinement and fuel injection. According to the company’s public statements, the AI models are trained on large datasets of plasma behavior, allowing the reactor to maintain stable fusion conditions with lower energy input than traditional designs.

Why Daniel Ek is Interested

Ek, who has a long history of investing in disruptive technology, said he was drawn to the startup’s potential to provide clean, virtually limitless energy. He noted that the fusion field has struggled to achieve a net‑positive energy output, but believes that AI can help overcome the remaining technical hurdles.

Funding and Future Plans

Europa Fusion has secured a seed round that includes Ek’s investment, bringing the total funding to $25 million. The company plans to use the capital to build a prototype reactor and begin testing in the next 18 months. If successful, the startup aims to license its technology to utilities and industrial customers.

Industry Context

Fusion power has long been seen as the holy grail of clean energy, but practical reactors have remained elusive. Recent advances in AI and high‑performance computing have opened new avenues for modeling complex plasma dynamics, and Europa Fusion is among the first startups to commercialize these advances.

Potential Impact

Should Europa Fusion achieve a net‑positive fusion reaction, it could dramatically reduce the cost of electricity and lower greenhouse gas emissions. The company’s approach also promises a smaller footprint compared to large tokamak projects, potentially accelerating deployment in urban areas.
