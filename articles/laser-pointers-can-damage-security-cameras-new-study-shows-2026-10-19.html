Laser Pointers Can Damage Security Cameras, New Study Shows

2026-10-19 · 3 min read
A recent analysis confirms that pointing laser pointers at security cameras can cause permanent damage, urging users to avoid the practice.

Security cameras are a staple of modern surveillance, yet a common hobbyist trick—pointing a laser pointer at them—has been shown to cause lasting harm. A new study from Engadget’s research team examined the effects of laser light on camera sensors and found that even brief exposure can degrade image quality and, in some cases, render a camera inoperable.

How Laser Light Affects Camera Sensors

The investigation focused on the photosensitive elements inside camera modules, which convert light into electrical signals. When a laser beam strikes these components, the concentrated energy can overheat the sensor’s micro‑electronics. The heat can damage the silicon lattice, leading to increased noise, reduced sensitivity, and permanent pixel loss.

Real‑World Impact on Security Systems

Security cameras are often installed in high‑traffic areas where accidental laser exposure is more likely. The study’s findings suggest that a single incident can compromise a camera’s ability to capture clear footage, potentially creating blind spots in critical monitoring zones.

Preventive Measures for Users

Experts recommend keeping laser pointers away from any optical devices. If you must use a laser pointer, ensure it is aimed at a non‑reflective surface and never direct it toward a camera lens. Additionally, installing cameras with built‑in laser protection—such as optical filters or sensor shielding—can mitigate the risk.

Industry Response

Camera manufacturers are taking note. Some are now incorporating laser‑resistant coatings on sensor surfaces, while others are advising users to follow best practices for laser use. The study’s authors hope that increased awareness will reduce accidental damage and extend the lifespan of surveillance equipment.
