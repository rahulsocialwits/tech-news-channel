Energy Companies Deploy Power‑Line‑Charging Drones to Monitor the Grid

2026-11-22 · 3 min read
Energy firms are testing drones that can land on live high‑voltage lines, draw power, and continue monitoring without returning to base.

Energy companies are turning to a new class of drones that can land on live high‑voltage power lines, draw power from the line itself, and then move on to the next task. The technology, dubbed NOMADs, promises to reduce the need for human crews to climb towers and to enable continuous monitoring of the grid.

How NOMADs Work

Unlike traditional drones that rely on batteries, NOMADs are equipped with a small, lightweight charging system that can connect to a high‑voltage line. When the drone lands, it establishes a safe electrical connection, draws the necessary power, and then lifts off again to continue its mission. Because the drone never has to return to a ground station, it can operate for extended periods, covering more of the grid than a battery‑powered counterpart.

Applications in Grid Monitoring

Energy utilities are using NOMADs to inspect transmission lines, detect faults, and gather data on line temperature and vibration. The drones can also deploy sensors that record environmental conditions, helping operators predict and prevent outages. By reducing the need for human inspections, utilities can lower costs and improve safety.

Deployment and Future Outlook

Several utilities have begun pilot programs in the United States and Europe. Early results show that the drones can operate in a variety of weather conditions and can be recharged on the fly, making them ideal for remote or hard‑to‑reach sections of the grid. As the technology matures, it is expected to become a standard tool for grid operators worldwide.
