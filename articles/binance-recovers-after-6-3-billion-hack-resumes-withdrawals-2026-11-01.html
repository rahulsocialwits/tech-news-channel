Binance Recovers After $6.3 Billion Hack, Resumes Withdrawals

2026-11-01 · 3 min read
The cryptocurrency exchange says it has covered 95% of stolen assets and is now allowing users to withdraw funds again.

Binance, the world’s largest cryptocurrency exchange, announced that it has resumed withdrawals after a massive security breach that stole roughly $6.3 billion in digital assets. The company said it has now covered 95% of the stolen funds, allowing users to reclaim their balances after a week of frozen accounts.

What Happened?

On October 31, Binance disclosed that hackers had exploited a vulnerability in its internal systems, siphoning off a significant portion of its user wallets. The breach was the largest in the crypto industry to date, according to reports from The Hindu and TechCrunch. While the exact method of the attack remains under investigation, the exchange confirmed that the stolen assets were primarily in Bitcoin and Ethereum.

Recovery Efforts

Binance’s response involved a multi‑layered approach. First, the exchange shut down all withdrawal functions to prevent further loss. Then, it launched an internal audit and engaged third‑party security firms to trace the stolen funds. The company also set up a dedicated recovery fund, which it claims will cover 95% of the missing assets. Users who had their withdrawals blocked are now able to request refunds or partial reimbursements through the platform’s support portal.

Impact on Users

The incident has shaken confidence in the security of centralized exchanges. Many users expressed frustration over the delay in accessing their funds, while others praised Binance for its transparency and swift action. According to a statement from Binance’s CEO, the company is working closely with regulators in multiple jurisdictions to ensure compliance and prevent future incidents.

Industry Reactions

Regulators in the United States and Europe have called for stricter oversight of crypto exchanges following the breach. The incident has also prompted discussions about the need for better security protocols, such as multi‑factor authentication and hardware‑based key storage. Some analysts argue that the scale of the hack underscores the importance of decentralization and the potential risks of relying on a single point of failure.

Looking Ahead

Binance has pledged to enhance its security infrastructure, including the implementation of advanced threat detection systems and regular penetration testing. The exchange also plans to increase transparency by publishing quarterly security reports. While the recovery of the remaining 5% of stolen assets remains uncertain, Binance’s swift action and public disclosure are seen as steps toward restoring user trust.
