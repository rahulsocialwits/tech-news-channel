Android 17 Adds Feature to Keep Audio in Headphones, Borrowing from iOS

2026-11-26 · 3 min read
Google’s latest Android 17 update introduces a setting that stops Bluetooth audio from switching to speakers when headphones are unplugged, a feature inspired by Apple’s iOS 26.

Google’s newest Android 17 release brings a small but useful tweak that many users will appreciate: a setting that keeps audio playing in headphones even when the device is unplugged. The feature, which mirrors a similar function in Apple’s iOS 26, prevents the sudden shift of sound to the phone’s built‑in speaker that can interrupt music or video playback.

What the update does

When the new setting is enabled, the phone will continue to route audio to the last connected Bluetooth headset until the user explicitly changes the output. This means that if you’re listening to a podcast and accidentally disconnect your headphones, the audio will not automatically switch to the phone’s speaker.

Why it matters

Bluetooth audio switching has long been a source of frustration for Android users. The change is expected to improve the listening experience for commuters, gamers, and anyone who relies on wireless headphones for media consumption.

How to enable it

After installing Android 17, go to Settings > Sound > Bluetooth audio. Toggle the new “Keep audio in headphones” option to activate the feature. The setting is available on all devices running Android 17 and later.
