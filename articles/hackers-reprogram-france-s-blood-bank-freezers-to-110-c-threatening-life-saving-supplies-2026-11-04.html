Hackers Reprogram France’s Blood Bank Freezers to 110°C, Threatening Life‑Saving Supplies

2026-11-04 · 3 min read
Cyber attackers altered the temperature settings of French blood bank freezers, raising them to 110°C and jeopardizing stored blood products.

In a chilling reminder of the growing sophistication of cyber‑attacks on critical infrastructure, hackers have reprogrammed the cold‑storage units of a French blood bank, pushing the temperature from the safe −30°C to a dangerous 110°C (230°F). The move, discovered by the bank’s security team, could have catastrophic consequences for the millions of blood products stored in the facility.

How the Attack Unfolded

The perpetrators exploited a vulnerability in the freezer’s firmware, allowing them to override the default temperature controls. Once the units were set to 110°C, the blood products began to degrade rapidly, rendering them unusable for transfusions. The bank’s monitoring system flagged the anomaly, prompting an immediate investigation.

Potential Impact on Healthcare

Blood banks rely on precise temperature maintenance to preserve the viability of red cells, platelets, and plasma. A rise to 110°C would denature proteins and destroy cellular structures, effectively destroying the stored supplies. Hospitals that depend on these products could face shortages, especially during emergencies or large-scale medical procedures.

Response from French Authorities

The French Ministry of Health has issued an emergency alert, urging all blood banks to audit their temperature control systems. Cybersecurity experts are advising the adoption of multi‑factor authentication and firmware integrity checks to prevent similar breaches. The incident has also prompted a review of the supply chain for medical equipment, highlighting the need for stricter oversight.

Broader Implications for Medical Cybersecurity

While the attack targeted a single facility, it underscores a broader threat to medical infrastructure worldwide. As hospitals increasingly adopt Internet‑of‑Things (IoT) devices for monitoring and automation, the attack surface expands. Experts warn that attackers can manipulate device settings to cause physical harm, a scenario that has been largely theoretical until now.

Preventive Measures for Blood Banks

  • Implement firmware verification protocols to detect unauthorized changes.
  • Use redundant temperature sensors and cross‑check readings against independent systems.
  • Establish rapid response teams to isolate affected units and restore safe conditions.
  • Conduct regular penetration testing focused on medical device controls.

By adopting these safeguards, blood banks can reduce the risk of future attacks and protect the life‑saving supplies they steward.
