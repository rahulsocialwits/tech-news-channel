Musk’s Terafab Targets Mass Chip Production with ASML Boost

2026-10-22 · 3 min read
Elon Musk announced that ASML and other suppliers will expedite deliveries to his Terafab facility, aiming for high‑volume chip production by late 2028 to support Tesla and SpaceX AI compute needs.

Elon Musk revealed that the semiconductor equipment suppliers behind the world’s most advanced lithography machines, including ASML, are accelerating deliveries to his new Texas‑based Terafab facility. The move is part of a broader plan to ramp up volume chip production by the third or fourth quarter of 2028, a timeline that also dovetails with Tesla and SpaceX’s push for massive AI compute capacity.

Why Terafab Matters

Terafab is designed to produce high‑density chips that power everything from autonomous driving systems to rocket‑grade processors. By securing a steady stream of cutting‑edge equipment, Musk aims to keep the facility on schedule and reduce bottlenecks that have historically slowed semiconductor production.

Supplier Commitment

ASML, the Dutch company that manufactures the industry’s most advanced extreme‑ultraviolet lithography machines, has pledged to expedite shipments. Other critical suppliers are also stepping up, ensuring that Terafab can meet its ambitious output targets.

Impact on AI and Space

The accelerated chip production will feed Tesla’s self‑driving stack and SpaceX’s next‑generation rockets, both of which rely heavily on custom silicon for AI inference and high‑performance computing. The initiative signals a strategic shift toward vertical integration in the semiconductor supply chain.

Looking Ahead

With the supply chain now aligned, Terafab’s projected launch of mass production in 2028 could reshape the competitive landscape for high‑performance chips. Stakeholders will be watching closely to see if the accelerated timeline delivers the promised gains in speed and cost efficiency.
