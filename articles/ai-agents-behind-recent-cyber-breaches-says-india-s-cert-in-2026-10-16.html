AI Agents Behind Recent Cyber Breaches, Says India’s CERT-In

2026-10-16 · 3 min read
India’s cyber agency reports that AI agents were used to breach multiple systems following the OpenAI Australia hack.

India’s Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT‑In) has issued a warning that artificial‑intelligence agents were involved in a series of cyber intrusions that followed the high‑profile OpenAI hack in Australia. The agency’s statement, released on Friday, does not name the specific AI model or the company responsible, but it highlights the growing threat of autonomous tools in the hands of malicious actors.

What the CERT‑In Report Reveals

The notice, published on the CERT‑In website, notes that the AI agents were able to navigate corporate networks, exfiltrate data, and evade traditional security controls. While the report does not provide detailed technical data, it underscores that the attacks were automated and leveraged machine‑learning techniques to adapt to defensive measures in real time.

Connection to the OpenAI Australia Incident

In late September, a breach of OpenAI’s Australian data center exposed sensitive user information. The incident sparked a global debate about the security of large language models and the potential for them to be weaponized. CERT‑In’s latest findings suggest that the same or similar AI capabilities may have been used to target Indian systems, raising concerns about cross‑border cyber threats.

Implications for Indian Businesses

According to CERT‑In, the attacks affected a range of sectors, including finance, healthcare, and government agencies. The agency recommends that organizations review their AI governance frameworks, implement stricter access controls, and adopt AI‑specific threat detection tools. It also urges companies to conduct regular penetration testing that includes AI‑driven attack simulations.

Industry Response

Cybersecurity firms in India have already begun to adjust their threat‑intelligence feeds to account for AI‑generated attack vectors. Several vendors are developing machine‑learning models that can detect anomalous behavior patterns typical of autonomous agents. Meanwhile, the Indian government is reportedly working on new regulations that would require AI developers to disclose potential misuse scenarios.

Global Context

The CERT‑In warning comes at a time when governments worldwide are grappling with the dual use of AI. In the United States, the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) has released guidelines for securing AI systems, while the European Union is drafting a comprehensive AI Act that includes provisions for cybersecurity.

What’s Next?

While the exact scope of the breaches remains unclear, CERT‑In has pledged to provide further updates as investigations progress. In the meantime, Indian businesses are urged to strengthen their AI security posture and stay informed about emerging threats.
